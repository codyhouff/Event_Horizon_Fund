# The Event Horizon Fund: A Strategic Audit and Optimization Report (January 2026)

## Executive Summary: The Physics of Intelligence

As of January 20, 2026, the artificial intelligence (AI) investment landscape has transitioned from a speculative frenzy into a period of industrial hardening. The "Event Horizon" thesis—which posits that value will accrue disproportionately to the physical bottlenecks of compute, power, and manufacturing—has not only been validated but has accelerated beyond initial 2024–2025 projections. The "AI-2027" forecast, once a theoretical roadmap for AGI capability, has become the operational reality for the world's largest capital allocators, driving a capital expenditure (capex) supercycle that is reshaping the global economy.

This report provides a comprehensive, forensic audit of the Event Horizon Fund (EHF). It evaluates the portfolio against the current macroeconomic and technological reality of early 2026, where the constraints on AI progress are no longer algorithmic but thermodynamic and logistical. The analysis confirms that the primary bottleneck has shifted from model architecture to the availability of 24/7 carbon-free baseload power, the thermal management of extreme-density server racks, and the yield rates of advanced packaging technologies like CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) and High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM).

### The Macro-Strategic Context: The \$3 Trillion Buildout

The total addressable market (TAM) for AI infrastructure has expanded at a velocity that defies historical precedent. In 2025 alone, the collective capital expenditure of the "Hyperscalers"—Microsoft, Amazon, Google, and Meta—exceeded \$320 billion, with projections for 2026 surpassing \$500 billion.<sup>1</sup> This expenditure is driving the construction of "AI Factories," gigawatt-scale data center campuses that consume as much power as medium-sized cities. The industry is witnessing the deployment of 100 GW of new capacity between 2026 and 2030, a physical buildout valued at over \$3 trillion.<sup>2</sup>

Crucially, the geopolitical dimension of this thesis has intensified. The "Chip War" has evolved into a "Power War" and a "Sovereignty War." The U.S. government's aggressive use of the CHIPS Act and tariffs has bifurcated the global supply chain, creating a distinct "sovereign premium" for assets domiciled in the United States or allied nations.<sup>3</sup> This dynamic has fundamentally altered the risk profile of semiconductor manufacturing, elevating companies like Intel and MP Materials from distressed value plays to strategic national assets.

### Portfolio Health & Thesis Validation

The EHF’s core thesis of owning the "hardest bottlenecks" has delivered substantial alpha in 2025.

- **Power (CEG):** Validated by the historic restart of Three Mile Island Unit 1 (now the Crane Clean Energy Center) for Microsoft and the Meta deal for the Clinton Clean Energy Center.<sup>4</sup> Nuclear power has re-rated from a utility commodity to a technology enabler.

- **Thermal (VRT):** Validated by the universal mandate for liquid cooling in Nvidia’s Blackwell architecture. Vertiv’s order backlog reached a record \$9.5 billion in late 2025, confirming its dominance.<sup>6</sup>

- **Silicon (TSM/NVDA):** Validated by TSMC’s aggressive 2026 capex guidance (\$52B–\$56B) and Nvidia’s multi-million unit backlog for Blackwell systems.<sup>7</sup>

- **The "Moonshot" Realized (INTC):** The most significant deviation from the original risk profile is Intel. With the confirmation of high-volume manufacturing for the 18A node and a foundry agreement with Apple, Intel has graduated from a speculative "Moonshot" to a foundational silicon holding.<sup>9</sup>

### Strategic Recommendation Overview

The analysis concludes that the EHF requires strategic rebalancing to optimize for the 2026–2027 window. The "Moonshot Sleeve," originally designed around complex options strategies (LEAPS), creates unnecessary friction and risk for the portfolio. It is recommended to convert this sleeve into direct equity positions and targeted leveraged ETFs, capturing the same asymmetric upside without the management overhead of derivatives. Furthermore, the allocation to Intel should be promoted to the core portfolio, while the position in Super Micro Computer (SMCI) should be liquidated in favor of Dell Technologies (DELL) due to the shift from "speed-to-market" to "scale-of-deployment."

### 1. Foundry & Advanced Packaging: The Indispensable Chokepoint

**Primary Holding: Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. (TSM)**

- **Target Allocation:** 12%

- **Status: Strong Hold**

Thesis Verification (Jan 2026):

The thesis that TSM is the "singular chokepoint" for AI progress remains irrefutable. As of Q1 2026, TSMC’s strategic importance has escalated due to its successful transition to the 2-nanometer (N2) process node. Yield rates for N2 are reportedly tracking ahead of internal projections, with mass production scheduled for late 2026 to support next-generation designs from Apple and Nvidia.11

The primary constraint—**CoWoS packaging**—continues to govern the global supply of AI compute. TSMC has committed to doubling its CoWoS capacity for the second consecutive year, projecting monthly capacity to reach 140,000–150,000 wafers by the end of 2026, up from ~80,000 in late 2025.<sup>12</sup> Despite this massive ramp, demand continues to outstrip supply. Nvidia alone has booked over 60% of TSMC’s 2026 CoWoS capacity to support its Blackwell (B200/B300) and upcoming Rubin architectures.<sup>13</sup> This chronic imbalance ensures TSMC retains immense pricing power, reflected in its gross margins climbing to 62.3% in Q4 2025.<sup>14</sup>

**Must-Be-True Threshold Status:**

- *Threshold:* "Must successfully ramp CoWoS capacity to \>50,000 wafers per month by end of 2026."

- *Status:* **Surpassed.** Current projections place capacity well above 100,000 wafers per month.<sup>12</sup>

**Trim Trigger Analysis:**

- *Trigger:* "Geopolitical conflict in Taiwan escalates."

  - *Assessment:* While tensions remain high, the "Silicon Shield" is intact. The U.S. CHIPS Act has facilitated the expansion of TSMC’s Arizona fabs, which are now critical for "Made in America" mandates from hyperscalers like Microsoft and Google, partially hedging the geopolitical risk.<sup>7</sup>

- *Trigger:* "Intel's foundry services (IFS) demonstrate a credible roadmap to match TSM's N2 node performance."

  - *Assessment:* Intel *has* demonstrated a credible roadmap with 18A (discussed below). However, the market is expanding fast enough to support both. TSMC’s dominance in packaging (CoWoS) remains unchallenged by Intel’s Foveros in the immediate term.

Optimization:

Maintain the 12% allocation. While Intel provides a domestic hedge, TSMC remains the volume leader for the entire AI ecosystem.
