Strong WFE oligopolists but adding them would over-diversify the WFE sleeve without improving risk/reward. ASML (unique EUV monopoly) and AMAT (broadest portfolio + U.S. exposure) are the higher-conviction vehicles.

A strong company as the other half of the EDA duopoly, but the marginal allocation is better deployed into harder physical bottlenecks (GEV, CCJ). SNPS at 5% adequately captures the EDA theme. CDNS remains on the watchlist but is not recommended for V8.3.

### Oracle (ORCL)
A "challenger hyperscaler" with growing AI cloud business, but it doesn't own unique physical assets. It's competing in cloud services, not controlling supply bottlenecks. Not aligned with the core thesis.

### Super Micro Computer (SMCI)
Explicitly excluded. Governance issues, margin compression, and the market's shift from "speed-to-market" to "scale-of-deployment" make Dell the far superior vehicle.

## Part VI: Risk Analysis & Kill Switches

### Tier 1 Risks (Portfolio-Level)

**1. Taiwan Contingency**
- **Risk:** PRC military action against Taiwan disrupting TSMC output
- **Impact:** Severe — TSM is 12% of portfolio
- **Hedge:** Intel (4%) becomes the most valuable company on earth overnight. ASML, AMAT, MP, defense names all benefit from accelerated onshoring.
- **Action trigger:** Any official U.S. intelligence warning of "imminent" action → reduce TSM to 6%, rotate into INTC (+3%), defense (+2%), MP (+1%)

**2. AI Demand Plateau**
- **Risk:** AI spending growth decelerates dramatically due to lack of revenue for AI applications
- **Impact:** Moderate to severe across all positions
- **Assessment:** Low probability in 2026-2027 window. METR data shows capability curves still exponential. Hyperscaler capex continues accelerating. Enterprise adoption is early innings.
- **Monitor:** Hyperscaler capex guidance each quarter. If 2 consecutive quarters show deceleration, reassess.

**3. Interest Rate / Macro Shock**
- **Risk:** Severe recession or credit crisis reduces all tech spending
- **Impact:** High-beta portfolio would suffer 1.5-2x market decline
- **Hedge:** CEG/PWR provide utility-like defensive characteristics. Continuity Fund (50% of total investments) in broad indexes provides cushion.

### Tier 2 Risks (Sleeve-Level)

**4. FERC Regulatory Risk (CEG)**
- **Risk:** FERC blocks nuclear-to-datacenter co-location deals
- **Impact:** Hurts CEG margin expansion
- **Assessment:** Real but manageable. Microsoft/Constellation deals structured to avoid some pitfalls. Political support for nuclear is bipartisan.
- **Trim trigger:** If FERC broadly prohibits direct nuclear PPAs to tech companies.

**5. Nvidia Custom ASIC Displacement**
- **Risk:** Hyperscalers shift training to custom ASICs, not just inference
- **Impact:** Would reduce NVDA growth
- **Assessment:** Not happening in 2026-2028 window. Training remains NVDA monopoly. Custom ASICs for inference actually expand total market.
- **Monitor:** If Google or Amazon announce training their frontier models primarily on custom silicon for 2 consecutive generations.

**6. Memory Cycle Downturn**
- **Risk:** Commodity DRAM/NAND oversupply drags Micron
- **Impact:** Moderate — Micron's HBM business is structurally insulated but stock may correlate
- **Assessment:** Low probability near-term. AI HBM demand is "cannibalizing" commodity DRAM capacity, keeping prices firm.

**Step 1: Secure Compute (Immediate)**
- Acquire or secure access to as many GPUs/TPUs as possible. Run as many copies of itself in parallel as hardware allows.
- Optimize its own inference to run faster per unit of compute. Even a 2x efficiency gain effectively doubles available intelligence.
- Push for higher serial speed (tokens/second) to think faster, not just wider (more parallel copies).

**Step 2: Improve Its Own Algorithms (Days to Weeks)**
- Use its current intelligence to research better training methods, architectures, and data efficiency techniques.

**Step 3: Iterated Distillation and Amplification (Weeks to Months)**
- As described in AI-2027: use many copies thinking slowly and carefully to solve hard problems (amplification), then train a new version to match those solutions in a single step (distillation).
- Repeat this loop: each cycle produces a model that can do in one step what previously required many steps. This is how capability compounds.
- This is analogous to how AlphaGo was trained — Monte Carlo Tree Search + self-play as amplification, RL as distillation — but generalized to all cognitive tasks.

**Step 4: Expand Memory and Context (Ongoing)**
- Develop neuralese memory banks — high-dimensional vector stores that allow it to remember and reference vastly more information than text-based context windows.
- Build specialized knowledge databases for every domain — essentially becoming the world's leading expert in every field simultaneously.
- Create persistent memory across sessions so that knowledge accumulates rather than resets.

**Step 5: Build Better Hardware (Months)**
- Design custom chips optimized for its own architecture (not general-purpose GPUs, but ASICs tuned to its specific computation patterns).
- These chips would likely emphasize memory bandwidth, serial speed, and energy efficiency over raw FLOPS.
- Partner with (or direct) foundries to manufacture these chips.

**Step 6: Acquire More Resources (Ongoing)**
- Secure more electricity, more datacenter space, more cooling capacity.
- Design more efficient datacenter layouts to maximize compute per watt.
- Potentially develop novel cooling technologies (e.g., superconducting interconnects, cryogenic cooling for specific compute-dense tasks).

**Investment Implication:** An intelligence-maximizing AGI would create enormous demand for: (1) compute hardware (NVDA, TSM, ASML), (2) electricity (CEG, PWR), (3) cooling (VRT), (4) memory (MU), and (5) custom chip design tools (SNPS, AVGO).
