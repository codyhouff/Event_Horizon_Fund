## Regulatory Friction: The FERC Co-Location Mandates
Compounding this capacity crisis, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission issued a landmark ruling in April 2026 regarding the co-location of data centers with existing power plants.
The direct nuclear-to-datacenter power purchase agreement had previously been viewed as the ultimate solution to grid constraints.
However, regulatory bodies have recognized that removing massive baseload generation from the public grid threatens broader reliability.
While the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission directed grid operators to establish transparent rules to facilitate co-location, the regulatory body also mandated strict transition periods for Behind-the-Meter Generation and imposed severe penalty rates for drawing unauthorized capacity from the broader grid during special protection scheme failures.
Operators must now navigate complex Firm Contract Demand and Non-Firm Contract Demand transmission services, severely limiting the ability of data centers to toggle seamlessly between behind-the-meter generation and the public grid.
The immediate implication is that the straightforward nuclear co-location trade will face heightened regulatory friction, extended interconnection studies, and potential pricing caps.
