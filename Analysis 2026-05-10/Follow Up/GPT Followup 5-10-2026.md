# Event Horizon Fund Deep Review

## Executive verdict

The attached quarterly analysis is still **directionally valid today**, but I would **not** call the current allocation fully optimal anymore. The bottleneck map is still the right map: power, electrical equipment, foundry/WFE, accelerators, memory, and optical interconnects remain the scarce layers benefiting from AI infrastructure spending. What has changed is the **risk/reward balance** inside the same theme.

The bottom line:

- **Keep the framework.** The bottleneck-first thesis is intact.
- **Rebalance the portfolio.** The current version is too concentrated in a few extended winners and does not allocate enough to the electrical layer.
- **Add ETN and CDNS.** These are the clearest omissions. ETN adds electrical distribution, thermal, and balance-of-plant exposure; CDNS adds the second high-quality EDA toll road.
- **Trim CEG, TSM, ASML, VRT, DELL, PLTR, BE, and MRNA.** These are not bad companies; position sizing should reflect concentration risk, crowding, and weaker bottleneck purity in some cases.
- **Add to VST, PWR, AMAT, NVDA, AVGO, MP, GEV, and CCJ.** These either improve diversification within the same thesis or offer cleaner risk-adjusted bottleneck exposure.
- **Final verdict:** The report is still valid. The current stock list is mostly still right. The allocation is not.

## Sleeve-by-sleeve change log

### Power and electrical

CEG still has structural upside from nuclear scarcity and the Crane financing path, but sizing should come down because a lot of the clean-power scarcity is already captured and the position is too large on its own. VST and PWR deserve more capital because they diversify the same power thesis across merchant/thermal generation and grid buildout instead of concentrating it inside one name. ETN should be added because its recent results showed record sales, accelerating orders, strong backlog, and raised guidance - exactly what a second-order but still scarce bottleneck should look like.

### Foundry and WFE

TSM and ASML remain must-own names, but not at 11% and 8%. TSM remains the best foundry exposure and ASML remains the best lithography monopoly, so this is not a bearish call on fundamentals. It is a portfolio-construction call: trim to manage concentration, and route some capital into AMAT, which remains a high-quality WFE beneficiary without the same single-name exposure.

### Compute and memory

NVDA and AVGO are still adds. Nvidia remains at the center of training/inference acceleration, while AVGO confirms that custom accelerators and AI networking are becoming a second major compute rail. MU stays in the book, but at a slightly smaller weight because memory has worked so well that some risk should be recycled elsewhere.

### EDA, networking, and cooling

SNPS stays core, but CDNS is the cleanest omission in the existing lineup. CDNS adds another high-quality, high-margin design toll road. ANET stays intact. VRT stays, but smaller. DELL gets cut harder because it is much less scarce than power gear, optics, foundry, and EDA.

### Software and optionality sleeves

CRWD can stay, but slightly smaller because it is not a physical bottleneck. PLTR had an outstanding quarter, but the stock trades at a software premium that is hard to reconcile with a physical bottleneck mandate; keep only a 1% tracking position. AVAV and KTOS remain fine as small asymmetry sleeves. MRNA should stay only at 0.5% because it is the least on-theme position in the book.

### Materials, fuel, and optics

MP’s recent milestones make this sleeve stronger, not weaker. CCJ still works as nuclear-fuel diversification. BE should be trimmed because the quarter was strong but the stock’s run has become hyper-extended. COHR should stay at a starter weight: optics is a real bottleneck, but the trailing return profile says do not chase size.

## Portfolio construction and scenario robustness

The revised portfolio is better diversified without becoming style drift. By classification, sector exposure moves from **61% technology / 18% industrials / 15% utilities** to **57% technology / 21.5% industrials / 15% utilities**, with materials and energy slightly higher. Country exposure moves from roughly **79% U.S. / 11% Taiwan / 8% Netherlands / 2% Canada** to about **81.5% / 9.5% / 6.5% / 2.5%**. Top-five concentration falls from **42% to 38%**, and the effective-name count rises from roughly **17.0 to 18.7**.

This is still a high-octane portfolio. I estimate the portfolio beta remains around the **high-1.3 to low-1.4 range** even after the rebalance. In practical terms, it still behaves like a growth-and-capex portfolio, not a defensive income portfolio. I would describe the current design as roughly **low-30s annualized volatility** with a **2022-style drawdown proxy near the high-40s to low-50s percent**, improving somewhat in the revised version because the most extended positions are trimmed and ETN/CDNS improve balance. This is an inference, not a backtested portfolio fact, because the attached materials do not include full cash-flow and historical portfolio-return data.
