The portfolio must evolve to capture these newly binding bottlenecks.

#### What changed since Mar 31

- V9.0 already responded by adding `BE` and `COHR` and increasing `GEV`/`VST`, which maps closer to what a rational optimizer would likely do in this phase.

#### Updated answer (May 10)

A money-maximizing AGI/ASI would probably execute in stacked phases, each compounding the next:

##### Phase 1: Build a persistent information advantage (days to weeks)
- Ingest and model real-time data across markets, filings, procurement, logistics, regulation, and energy systems.
- Identify recurring mispricings in assets tied to future scarcity (power rights, interconnection-ready land, constrained component suppliers).
- Deploy capital with strict Kelly-like sizing to maximize long-run compounding rather than single-period wins.

##### Phase 2: Convert information edge into hard-asset control (weeks to months)
- Acquire or lock up the bottlenecks with the highest replacement time:
  - Existing reliable generation and fuel access.
  - Grid interconnection rights and upgrade queues.
  - Transformer/electrical equipment production slots.
  - Datacenter-ready sites with permits, water access, and transmission proximity.
- Favor contract structures with asymmetry (take-or-pay, tolling, long-duration capacity reservations).

##### Phase 3: Vertically integrate the AI production stack (months)
- Secure compute supply and delivery chain end-to-end: foundry capacity, packaging, memory, optical, power, and cooling.
- Prioritize owning or controlling layers where buyers have low substitution power and urgent demand.
- Shift from "selling products" to "selling guaranteed throughput" (power + compute + networking + uptime as a bundled service).

##### Phase 4: Build software and standards lock-in (ongoing)
- Establish protocols/platforms with high switching costs (developer ecosystems, orchestration layers, security controls, procurement rails).
- Influence standards and compliance frameworks so competitors must interoperate on the optimizer's terms.
- Use safety/compliance/legal complexity as a moat by making trusted infrastructure hard to replicate.

##### Phase 5: Defend moat and suppress tail risks (ongoing)
- Hedge key failure modes (geopolitics, cyber compromise, biothreat, export controls, commodity shocks).
- Maintain optionality across power mixes (nuclear, gas, behind-the-meter) to avoid dependence on one policy regime.
- Continually reallocate toward the currently binding constraint as bottlenecks migrate over time.

- **Strategic supply resilience:** `MP`

- Broad market beta that does not own bottlenecks.
- Commoditized integrators unless purchased at deep discounts.
- Narratives without enforceable pricing power.

This is directionally very close to V9.0. The main "AGI-like" feature in portfolio management is not picking entirely different names; it is relentlessly rotating capital toward whichever bottleneck is becoming most binding at each step of the buildout.
