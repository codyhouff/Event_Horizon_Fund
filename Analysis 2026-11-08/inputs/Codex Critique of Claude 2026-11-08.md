## 1. Verdict

- **Claude's best addition is Micron.** Its longer-duration customer commitments justify more durable earnings than my original memory normalization allowed. At the same November 9 price, my new MU estimate is 12.2% expected CAGR, versus Coherent's 8.8%; AI-2027 returns are 20.1% versus 14.6%. I replace COHR's entire 8% with MU. This does not require accepting Claude's $130 terminal EPS, embedded buybacks, or the idea that memory is no longer cyclical.
- **Claude was closer on ASML's operating opportunity.** My old earnings bridge understated the capacity expansion. ASML now beats my preferred excluded equipment challenger, Lam Research: 12.3% versus 8.4% expected CAGR. It nevertheless loses the last capital slot to MU under the required higher-AI-scenario-return tie-break.
- **Claude's weakest holding is Kratos.** The independently reconstructed common-earnings, dilution and investment bridge gives approximately −1.3% expected CAGR, versus Claude's repriced 9.9%. A good procurement story does not establish a good common-stock return. The proposed defense/materials/biotech sleeve also is not demonstrated catastrophe insurance.
- **Claude's caution on Broadcom and Coherent improves my underwriting.** I reduce terminal multiples and common-share economics and explicitly charge Broadcom's financing exposure. But contingent lease support is not the same as an immediately payable face-value debt obligation. The financing charge alone does not justify a full AVGO exit; the combined return comparison does.
- **My strongest retained disagreements are CLS, NVT and NRG.** Claude's zero weights leave substantial estimated return on the table after current guidance, organic growth, sale-related earnings loss, capital spending and debt are accounted for. I retain 12% each, not because these are mandatory bottleneck sleeves.
- **Verisk, which I underwrote but excluded and Claude never considered, now displaces Broadcom.** Its unchanged operating model, repriced to November 9, offers 15.0% expected CAGR versus AVGO's re-underwritten 12.1%. Its AI-2027 return is also slightly higher. The funding is AVGO 15% → VRSK 15%; this is not a concession to Claude or a token hedge.
- **Revised own allocation:** NVDA 15%, VRT 15%, VRSK 15%, CLS 12%, NVT 12%, NRG 12%, VST 11%, MU 8%. Checksum **100.0%**. Using identical new underwriting, expected CAGR improves from 17.3% to 17.9%, AI-2027 CAGR from 22.3% to 22.7%, and the simultaneous-bust terminal loss improves from 65.0% to 57.9%. The 100GW CAGR falls from 44.9% to 42.5%: I accept that cost because expected return is primary.
- **This is a conditional, fragile improvement, not a proven optimum.** A 20% reduction in Verisk's terminal P/Es can erase the expected-return advantage. Eight holdings still concentrate on common AI spending, power and financial-market risks. Step 3 must compare these revised estimates with the remaining previously underwritten universe at the same prices and assumptions; this bounded cross-critique does not certify that every non-contested challenger loses.

### Complete current-price outcome ledger

Terminal wealth includes terminal common-equity value plus cumulative nominal dividends; no dividend reinvestment, taxes or trading costs are assumed. Expected wealth is calculated first, then annualized. Portfolio returns use weighted wealth ratios, not an average of stock CAGRs. Values are rounded for display; the companion JSON retains calculation precision.

`E[W] = Σ(probability × terminal wealth)`; `Expected CAGR = (E[W] / current price)^(1 / 4.1424) − 1`.

The default company financial-state probabilities are 25/50/25. KTOS, MP, BNTX and SNDK use 30/50/20 because dilution, project/clinical outcomes or commodity normalization create a less favorable distribution. These are subjective company-financial judgments, not claimed frequencies or the probabilities of particular AGI dates. They are not obtained by counting how many historical AI predictions came true.

Debt is not subtracted a second time from an already levered earnings valuation. Conversely, excess-cash additions must exclude earnings from that cash to avoid double counting.
