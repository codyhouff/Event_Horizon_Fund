## 1. Executive verdict

### Recommendation: concentrate in cash-generating infrastructure at prices that compensate for the cycle

Choose the **eight-stock concentrated architecture**: NVDA 15%, AVGO 15%, VRT 15%, CLS 12%, NVT 12%, NRG 12%, VST 11%, COHR 8%. Total **100.0%**. Keep Event Horizon at approximately 50% of net investments, leave Foundation and Continuity unchanged, and keep Moonshot at 0%. No options, leverage, shorts, private securities, cryptocurrencies, or ETFs are recommended.

The decisive change is not a prediction that AGI arrives on a particular date. It is a move from owning an inventory of strategic bottlenecks to owning a smaller set of favorable business-price combinations. There is no requirement to hold a foundry, lithography vendor, defense contractor, vaccine developer, uranium miner, or security platform merely because its industry matters. Nineteen of the 24 baseline holdings receive a zero target; three new names enter. Five existing positions remain, all at increased target weights.

The model implies approximately **135% expected cumulative total return**, or **22.8% annualized**, over 4.151 years. The current allocation produces approximately 61.5% and 12.2%; the 15-stock core-and-satellite alternative produces approximately 113.7% and 20.1%. These are subjective scenario calculations, not consensus forecasts, backtested alpha, or assurances of the highest realized return. A sensible interpretation is a high-teens-to-mid-20s opportunity, with substantial model error, rather than confidence in the decimal.

The concentrated portfolio's simultaneous bear-state terminal loss is approximately **57%**. A severe interim drawdown could be worse. Taiwan interruption, systemic credit stress, an AI investment bust, or a major failure at one of the largest positions could permanently impair capital. The 15% stock limit means an individual total loss costs 15% of Event Horizon, approximately 7.5% of total net investments before overlapping Continuity exposure. Continuity is another equity portfolio, not a capital guarantee.

### What changes the investment decision

1. **Price and fundamentals have diverged.** The hypothetical May baseline lost approximately 12.27% through November 6, despite substantial operating progress at many constituents. VRT is a particularly clear example: a lower price and higher earnings guidance materially improve its valuation. Buying it now is a different decision from paying May's multiple.
2. **The low-multiple challengers deserve real consideration.** CLS combines an ambitious new 2027 earnings outlook with an unusually low multiple; NRG offers a much lower earnings entry price than the nuclear-premium alternatives; NVT provides electrical distribution exposure below several better-known equipment names. Their discounts have identifiable risks, not simply market irrationality.
3. **Memory profits are extraordinary, not demonstrably permanent.** MU's current earnings power is much higher than in May, but the model explicitly normalizes margins and reserves substantial cash. A low peak-cycle P/E is not enough to displace the preferred eight.
4. **The strongest past winner was not evidence for the stated hedge.** MRNA's enormous gain coincided with oncology progress. That does not validate the claim that a small vaccine holding reliably insures against an engineered pandemic. At the new price, the required commercial success is materially greater.
5. **Financing is now a bottleneck alongside power and chips.** Growth capex, GPU replacement, customer concentration, equity issuance, acquisition liabilities, and deposits complicate headline earnings. The neoclouds remain investable common stocks, but none passes the final capital-allocation test here.

## 3. Zero-based portfolio test and bottleneck map

### 3.1 Start without sleeve entitlements

The blank-slate process is: identify scarce inputs; determine who can retain economic rents; translate operating scenarios into fully diluted per-share value; compare expected terminal wealth at today's price; then apply permanent-loss constraints. Only afterward compare the result with V9.1. The initial universe included the prior watchlists, hyperscalers, equipment competitors, electrical suppliers, optics specialists, neoclouds, data/identity platforms, and non-AI alternatives.

The candidate ranking is not based on recent winners or on each stock's old portfolio weight. The selected eight would be the same starting from cash under these assumptions. In particular, TSM and ASML receive zero despite exceptional strategic positions; MRNA receives zero despite being the largest contributor; and VRT receives the maximum default weight despite being the largest detractor. These outcomes distinguish zero-based capital competition from thesis-preserving rebalancing.

### 3.3 The background sources are scenario inputs, not investment evidence by themselves

*AI 2027* is an explicitly speculative scenario with named fictional entities and sharply rising uncertainty; its calendar is not a verified industry roadmap. *Situational Awareness* is a June 2024 thesis about compute, industrial mobilization and security, not a November 2026 forecast or valuation source. Both are **STALE background**. Their useful contribution is to identify dependencies and discontinuities, not to justify unlimited terminal multiples. [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/), [Situational Awareness](https://situational-awareness.ai/).

The original METR work measured success on tasks categorized by human completion time. That is not the duration for which a model can safely run an organization. Crucially, METR's time-horizon page added a September 8, 2026 notice that it is no longer actively updated, and it warns that measurements above 16 hours are unreliable with that suite. Treat the original March 2025 seven-month-doubling narrative as **STALE, bounded empirical evidence**, not a continuously verified exponential law. [Original study](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/), [methodology and update history](https://metr.org/time-horizons/).

Epoch's live trends page, retrieved November 8, distinguishes frontier training compute from aggregate new chip compute and efficiency. It reports approximately 2.5× annual growth in newly sold compute over its recent window, versus a faster earlier period, and improving chip performance per dollar. The plotted chip series extends through Q2 2026 and is therefore **STALE as an operating observation**, even when the page is freshly retrieved. These different rates cannot be multiplied together indiscriminately to forecast revenue. [Epoch trends and underlying chart dates](https://epoch.ai/trends).

The evidence supports substantial infrastructure demand without establishing an inevitable 100-GW incremental US AI build by 2030. The 100-GW premise is a scenario to stress, not an asset that already exists. The underwriting therefore does not require AGI in 2027, yet remains exposed to a much slower spending trajectory.

### 4.3 Valuation conventions — reproducible, not black-box price targets

All terminal scenarios are this report's **own November 8 assumptions**, not company guidance or consensus. The endpoint is December 31, 2030. The elapsed period from the November 6 close is 4.150582 years. Revenue, profits and share counts are in USD billions unless stated otherwise. ASML and CCJ are modeled in USD; the base translation conventions are $1.10 per euro and $0.73 per Canadian dollar, **model assumptions rather than claimed spot rates**. RELX's model is USD per ADR, with one ordinary-share-equivalent unit; any execution requires confirming the ADR terms and FX.

RELX's one-for-one ADS ratio is supported by its [2025 security description filed in 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/929869/000110465926017277/relx-20251231xex2d4.htm), a **STALE structural reference** checked for instrument consistency. The model does not confuse RELX with the unrelated RLX Technology ticker.

For earnings models:

`terminal stock value = 2030 revenue × sustainable after-tax common margin ÷ diluted shares × terminal P/E`

`terminal total wealth = terminal stock value + cumulative 2027–2030 dividends + explicitly modeled excess cash`

For EV models:

`terminal stock value = max(0, EBITDA × EV/EBITDA − net debt − preferred/NCI claims) ÷ diluted shares`

For CRWD/PANW, the margin is **owner FCF after an economic SBC charge**, and the multiple is P/owner-FCF. Residual share growth is also allowed for; repurchase-funded compensation is not treated as free money. For other earnings models, sustainable margins are after interest and taxes, and after a normalized compensation burden. Acquisition amortization is normalized only where replacement investment and cash economics warrant it; reported adjusted EPS is not blindly capitalized.

`expected terminal wealth = p_bear × TV_bear + p_base × TV_base + p_bull × TV_bull`

`implied expected CAGR = (expected terminal wealth ÷ current price)^(1/4.150582) − 1`

Scenario probabilities sum to 100% for each stock. **Scenario CAGRs are never averaged.** This CAGR is the annualized growth rate to expected terminal wealth; it is not the expectation of the investor's realized CAGR, which is a different nonlinear quantity. Dividends are accumulated without reinvestment, making forward total-wealth calculations slightly conservative relative to identical successful reinvestment. Historical adjusted-close returns use a reinvestment proxy, so the two conventions are disclosed rather than silently mixed.

Terminal multiples are normative underwriting ranges, not asserted historical medians. Their lower end allows ordinary cyclical/commodity economics; the upper end requires durable moats and long remaining growth. The May-versus-November valuations above provide verified within-cycle history where possible. **Reliable complete five- or ten-year multiple percentiles are unavailable**; a two-date comparison is not a substitute for that database. Peer comparisons use like economic measures as far as possible and explicitly flag adjusted earnings, lease/capex intensity, merger perimeter and FX differences.

The model uses representative bear/base/bull values inside much wider plausible ranges. Display rounding is not confidence. Bear probabilities rise to 30–40% for fragile, capital-intensive or technology-transition businesses; bull probabilities fall to 20%. These are judgmental, not frequencies estimated from a large historical sample. Catastrophic total loss remains possible even for companies with a positive modeled bear value.

TLN's corresponding normalized owner cash is about $1.7–2.0bn in the base scenario.

For GPU lessors, EBITDA margins exclude the most important economic cost: replacement hardware. Base CRWV/NBIS/IREN cases require post-interest, post-tax, post-replacement owner cash margins of roughly 5–15%, after funding the modeled debt and dilution. Their EV multiples are therefore capped well below software multiples. CORZ/APLD, which are more colocation-oriented, require approximately 15–25% owner cash margins after recurring facility investment and financing; tenant credits, preferred equity and noncontrolling claims still belong ahead of or beside common owners. These are feasibility constraints on the terminal scenarios, not additional uncounted cash to add to equity value.

### 4.5 Current-holding underwriting notes

Each note addresses why an apparent discount exists, whether it is likely temporary or structural, the relevant peer/history context, a catalyst, and a falsifier. Numerical revenue, earnings, dilution, dividends and terminal returns are in the preceding ledger; the following judgments explain why those assumptions were chosen.

**TSM — exceptional business, fair stock, 0%.** Relative to its May price it is cheaper, but the discount cannot be separated from geographic concentration and extraordinary ongoing capital requirements. The base assumes roughly $310bn revenue, a 45% common margin and 5.2bn ADR-equivalent diluted shares by 2030; owner FCF is below earnings because fabs require reinvestment. A 23× terminal multiple gives meaningful growth credit without assuming geopolitical risk vanishes. GFS/Samsung/INTC do not match its leading-node economics. Catalysts are successful node/packaging ramps and durable customer commitments. Falsifiers are sustained yield or utilization deterioration, a materially worse geographic risk regime, or overseas expansion earning persistently inadequate returns. The stock is not a value trap in the ordinary operating sense; it is a risk-bearing asset whose expected return loses to customers and suppliers at these prices.

**NVDA — attractive, 15%.** The discount reflects fears about AI return on investment, custom ASIC substitution, export restrictions, customer financing and peak margins. Some concerns are structural; the portfolio does not assume they disappear. The base reduces the terminal earnings multiple to 25× and supports approximately $850bn revenue with a 46% normalized common margin, not perpetual 100% growth. Net share count falls only modestly; cash buys back stock rather than becoming a second terminal asset. FCF conversion is assumed near earnings because it is fabless, subject to inventory and ecosystem investment needs. AMD is a credible technical rival but has a less forgiving valuation; AVGO is complementary. Catalyst: useful workload demand and per-rack delivery become recurring cash. Falsifiers: gross margin below 65% for two quarters without a temporary ramp explanation, material uncollectible receivables, or structurally worsening price/performance competitiveness.

The [August 27 Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm), **STALE**, makes the financing risk concrete: supply commitments reached $279bn and a new class of AI-cloud agreements involved $36bn of commitments, generally over six years, reduced as capacity is consumed. These are not all immediate debt and must not be subtracted blindly from equity value; they are also not costless demand creation. The model assumes adequate utilization and ordinary cash conversion. A material cash call or unused committed capacity would invalidate that assumption and require lower margins/cash conversion, potentially lower weight. The 15% target is a cap, not a declaration that fabless means capital-risk-free.

**AVGO — attractive, 15%.** The market discounts a small set of major AI customers, project timing, possible customer insourcing, acquisition accounting and leverage. The base's 50% common margin is aggressive and only credible if software and high-value silicon retain substantial economics; it is not a claim that all hardware companies deserve that margin. The normalized measure charges recurring compensation and maintenance economics, rather than using every management add-back. Revenue of $250bn and a 24× multiple are enough to support the modeled return without an unlimited re-rating. FCF services debt, dividends and modest buybacks, so the same cash is not counted twice. Catalyst: diversified custom-silicon ramps plus software cash realization. Falsifiers: an anchor program loss, AI growth below 20% across two years, materially rising net debt despite strong reported cash flow, or persistent common-margin compression below the mid-40s.

**MU — seemingly cheap peak earnings, fair normalized opportunity, 0%.** The market is not obliged to put a software multiple on a memory supercycle. The base allows $240bn revenue but normalizes net margin to 40%; bear margin is 20%, bull 50%. Even these levels are strong and need HBM mix and discipline. The ledger separately reserves $150/share of terminal excess cash in the base, avoiding the opposite mistake of ignoring extraordinary current cash generation. Capital expansion consumes much of the rest; the share count does not simultaneously assume heroic repurchases. SK hynix is an important competitor, not a reason to model global memory as a monopoly. Catalyst: cash accumulation persists after meaningful new supply arrives. Falsifiers: HBM pricing/qualification weakens, inventory rises while lead times shrink, or industry additions force rapid margin reversion. A durable cash-return plan could make MU a future replacement; “sold out” does not establish that outcome through 2030.

**ASML — excellent scarcity, fair-to-rich valuation, 0%.** Its moat is qualitatively stronger than most holdings, but the stock already prices a very substantial earnings increase. The base implies about $70bn revenue and 34% net margin in USD, versus a terminal multiple of 30×. That is not a pessimistic view. Current euro financials and the ADR price must be translated consistently; no supposed cheapness from mixing currencies is accepted. The peer comparison is process-equipment cyclicality, not a claim that another vendor can replace EUV. Customer capacity planning and installation schedules can move revenue across years. Catalyst: shipment/service growth with strong cash realization. Falsifiers: prolonged customer capex cuts, export restrictions impairing service, or a technical roadmap that reduces expected lithography intensity. Its 8.6% modeled return is insufficient for this mandate despite its strategic rank.

**AMAT — fair, 0%.** Lower price and stronger operating earnings improve the setup, but export exposure, customer capex and competitive process share are structural risks. Base revenue $50bn, 28% common margin, modest buybacks and a 24× terminal multiple are already constructive. FCF conversion is high after ordinary equipment-business reinvestment, not customer-fab spending. LRCX's faster earnings scaling is the better current equipment challenger; KLAC offers inspection quality. Catalyst: sustained process intensity and broad equipment/service demand. Falsifiers: share loss in key process steps, weak services resilience, or margin erosion despite rising system volumes. No need to own both AMAT and ASML for narrative completeness.

**SNPS — fair, 0%.** The de-rating creates a credible opportunity, but Ansys integration, debt, issuance and the gap between reported and adjusted earnings warrant discipline. The August balance sheet showed approximately $10bn debt and material goodwill/intangibles; the model does not value acquisition revenue as free organic growth. Base diluted shares remain around 200m, allowing more ownership than a pre-acquisition share count. Owner FCF is discounted for integration and compensation. CDNS is cleaner in some respects but more expensive against this model. Catalyst: debt reduction and cross-selling with sustained renewal strength. Falsifiers: organic bookings weakness, poor integration returns, repeated dilution without commensurate per-share growth, or AI tools undermining monetization faster than they expand demand. A better business than many selected companies can still be a worse stock at the margin.

**ANET — fair, 0%.** Strong recent operating execution justifies revising overly bearish competitive claims. The reason for excluding it is valuation-adjusted opportunity cost, not a prediction that Ethernet loses. Base revenue $30bn and 35% net margin with a 30× terminal P/E represent a very successful business. No extra terminal cash windfall is assumed on top of an already premium multiple; share dilution is modest. NVDA networking, custom switch silicon, open systems and customer concentration all limit unconstrained pricing. Catalyst: large-scale Ethernet deployments with stable economics. Falsifiers: structural share loss, chronic gross-margin pressure or a customer concentration event. AVGO supplies broader earnings exposure at the stronger modeled return.

**VRT — attractive after a major reset, 15%.** The May valuation concern has substantially dissipated without an equivalent decline in the earnings outlook. That is the strongest kind of price decline to investigate, but orders can include duplication and customers can defer delivery. Base revenue $30bn, 20% common margin and 26× earnings require operational maturity rather than a permanent shortage multiple. Normalized owner FCF is 85% of earnings after capacity/working-capital needs. Modine and nVent are real competitors; VRT's service, qualification and power/thermal integration support a premium. Catalyst: backlog converts into cash at stable margins. Falsifiers: two quarters of book-to-bill below one alongside cancellations, a persistent adjusted operating margin below 20%, or deteriorating cash conversion not explained by a demonstrably profitable ramp.

**DELL — attractive headline multiple, less attractive normalized return, 0%.** The low guided P/E is genuine, but the appropriate question is how much AI hardware revenue becomes sustainable common earnings and cash. The base assumes $300bn revenue but only 5.5% net margin, versus much stronger short-term reported earnings; component pass-through and mix can reverse. FCF conversion is modeled at 65% of earnings because working capital is economically important. Buybacks reduce shares only gradually and must compete with debt and inventory funding. HPE is a viable comparable; CLS's modeled growth-to-price ratio is better. Catalyst: repeat AI orders with sustained post-working-capital cash. Falsifier: AI growth expands receivables/inventory much faster than cash profits or produces margin compression. May's “integrator” label was not enough analysis; the new zero is an explicit valuation decision.

**CEG — fair, 0%.** Existing nuclear capacity is valuable, but this is not simply an untouched nuclear asset with riskless AI pricing. Acquisition perimeter, integration, financing, outages, hedging and political limits to ratepayer costs must be considered. The base common margin and 22× terminal P/E deliberately exclude a perpetual scarcity premium. The November 9 report is a near-term data gate, not information available here. VST and NRG offer better modeled entry economics; TLN is the closest concentrated nuclear challenger. Catalyst: contracted output converts to higher durable earnings without adverse policy or operating surprises. Falsifiers: outages or integration costs consume expected gains, or political/contracting arrangements prevent rent capture. Nuclear strategic importance is not disputed by a zero weight.

**VST — attractive, 11%.** The EV model recognizes both valuable generating assets and substantial financial claims. It does not capitalize derivative mark-to-market gains. Base EBITDA reaches $11bn, net claims fall to $20bn, and diluted shares to 300m; these outcomes must be financed from cash after recurring investment, not from unlimited leverage. The 9× terminal EV multiple is deliberately ordinary. Lower realized margins and weaker deleveraging produce a large bear loss despite owning physical assets. Catalyst: contracted cash, Cogentrix integration and disciplined capital returns. Falsifiers: net leverage stays above roughly 3.5× through a strong operating period, acquisitions outrun cash generation, or post-growth cash falls persistently below the underwriting bridge. NRG is cheaper; VST supplies a different fleet/retail mix and reduces dependence on one power company's execution.

**PWR — fair, 0%.** It is a high-quality way to own the people and execution needed for infrastructure. Yet the new lower price still buys a large future margin and scale improvement. Base revenue $60bn and 8% net margin, plus 25× earnings, yield only about 10.9% modeled CAGR. Booked work is not free cash: labor, claims, customer terms, acquisition costs and equipment investment matter. MYRG and MTZ offer related exposure, not riskless cheaper substitutes. Catalyst: backlog converts with stable project economics and cash. Falsifiers: margin deterioration, project losses or working capital rising structurally faster than revenue. The final portfolio deliberately accepts less direct EPC exposure because electrical and cooling equipment provide stronger return estimates.

**CRWD — expensive, 0%.** The split-adjusted price is essential: using a pre-split EPS with a post-split price would fabricate cheapness. The thesis of rising security demand is intact, but owner FCF after compensation is much less than an unadjusted FCF headline. The base allows $16bn revenue, a 23% owner-FCF margin and a generous 35× cash multiple; it still does not support the current price. PANW is relatively less expensive but also fails the final return hurdle. Catalyst for reconsideration: materially faster durable revenue and lower dilution, or a substantially lower price. Falsifier of the zero: recurring owner cash per share compounds well above this model without an offsetting competitive or reliability cost. No “insurance” label can justify ignoring valuation.

**PLTR — expensive, 0%.** The operating improvement is real and substantial. The base nevertheless requires $28bn revenue, a 38% common margin and 40× terminal earnings; the bull requires $45bn and 45% margin. Those are already favorable assumptions. Client data remain client data, not a proprietary asset that Palantir can freely resell. SBC and the fully diluted share denominator cannot be omitted. MSFT is the stronger diversified platform challenger. Catalyst for reconsideration: durable large contracts and cash economics that exceed the model, with dilution constrained. Falsifiers: deal growth stalls, implementation burdens rise, government concentration increases, or the high-margin growth narrative fails to reach owners. Conversely, exceptional current growth does not prove every valuation is rational.

**AVAV — speculative, 0%.** The stock is cheaper after a difficult period, but the discount reflects integration, accounting complexity and cash conversion as well as sentiment. Base revenue $4bn, 10% common margin and 60m diluted shares allow a successful business without assuming all announced military demand becomes high-margin orders. LHX/CACI offer more established economics; none has shown a sufficiently superior AI-linked return to warrant a sleeve. Catalyst: funded backlog, delivery execution and ordinary free cash flow. Falsifiers: repeated integration charges, weak funded conversion, further dilution or a major contract/program loss. A diversified defense budget is not the same as a diversified equity cash flow.

**KTOS — speculative, 0%.** Program announcements and engineering credibility precede production revenue and profits, sometimes by years. The base requires $3.8bn revenue, a 7% net margin and a materially larger share count. Even a 28× multiple produces little expected advantage at today's price. The cash-use outlook makes the discount partly structural rather than simply temporary market pessimism. Catalyst: recurring funded production at attractive unit economics and positive FCF. Falsifiers: program delays, cancellations, repeated capital raises, or unmanned revenue failing to scale. The model does not assume a particular military outcome or procurement winner.

**MRNA — speculative at a transformed price, 0%.** Positive oncology evidence genuinely increases business value, but rapid stock appreciation also increases the revenue and margin hurdle. The base assumes $12bn 2030 revenue, a 25% margin and 450m diluted shares; bull $25bn and 35% margin, with separate limited residual cash. That valuation must respect partner economics, manufacturing capacity, reimbursement, launches, R&D and continued financing. BNTX and diversified diagnostics challenge the assertion of a unique hedge. Catalyst: approvals and repeatable commercial economics, not a hypothetical catastrophe. Falsifiers: delayed or narrower approvals, poor uptake, manufacturing difficulty, cash burn or dilution beyond the model. Selling is not denying the scientific progress; it is rejecting a forward return and insurance claim at $254.19.

**MP — speculative, 0%.** The apparent bargain after a 47% fall rests on magnet ramp economics and policy-supported processing, not current common profit. The base assumes $2bn revenue and 22% net margin, allowing substantial success; it also allows dilution. Government support may reduce project risk without eliminating common-share risk, and one-off materials sales should not be annualized as recurring economics. FCX is a broad copper investment; Lynas a different rare-earth exposure. Neither solves gallium by owning MP. Catalyst: qualified magnet sales scale with positive cash after expansion. Falsifiers: poor yields/customer acceptance, policy changes, or sustained dependence on external funding. A floor under product pricing is not a floor under the stock.

**INTC — speculative/value-trap risk, 0%.** A much lower price is not enough when leading-edge foundry economics remain unproven and capital consumption is substantial. The base allows $85bn revenue and 10% common margin but also 5.8bn shares; the bull allows a genuine recovery. The bear $6 is a judgmental residual value, not a state guarantee. TSM is the operational comparator; GFS the more proven external-foundry model at different nodes. Catalyst: externally funded, profitable customer volume with credible process yields. Falsifiers: customer announcements never become revenue, capital requirements force repeated dilution, or internal transfers are mistaken for external demand. A Taiwan crisis could damage Intel's ecosystem and customers too; the purported hedge is unreliable.

**GEV — strategically strong, fair-to-rich normalized stock, 0%.** The discount reflects doubts about how much backlog and deposit-rich cash flow becomes sustainable profit. Deposits fund future obligations and cannot all be distributed without affecting delivery. Base earnings use $75bn revenue and 13% net margin, with normalized owner cash at 80% of earnings, rather than capitalizing the current exceptional FCF guide forever. Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi are real turbine competitors; equipment scarcity is not a literal monopoly. Catalyst: margins and delivery execution catch up with bookings. Falsifiers: warranty/service costs, customer cancellations, deposit reversals or weak cash conversion after the order wave. NVT and ETN offer stronger modeled valuation-adjusted alternatives.

**CCJ — expensive on normalized owner economics, 0%.** The stock is not a direct spot-uranium instrument. Contract timing, realized pricing, production costs, purchases and Westinghouse earnings create a complicated bridge to cash. The model converts the economics to USD and allows $5bn revenue and 25% net margin in the base; these are constructive assumptions. Kazatomprom adds jurisdiction/transport issues; LEU adds enrichment execution and policy risk. Catalyst: production reliability and higher-priced contracts translate to distributable cash. Falsifiers: outages force expensive purchases, cost inflation absorbs pricing, or new-reactor expectations do not become orders. Fuel scarcity alone does not justify the entry multiple.

**BE — speculative, 0%.** Revenue and margin progress are important, yet the common share price still requires large success. The base allows $10bn revenue and 15% net margin but 360m shares; the bull allows much more. Working capital, capex, service obligations and financing prevent adjusted operating earnings from equaling distributable cash. Gas connections, air permits, fuel price and equipment reliability remain relevant. CMI/CAT and grid-delivered generation are substitutes with different deployment constraints. Catalyst: commissioned customer capacity at profitable lifetime economics. Falsifiers: warranty degradation, delayed acceptance, adverse fuel economics, permitting problems or repeated capital raises. Announced multi-GW frameworks are not the same as delivered, paid, high-margin systems.

**COHR — attractive but cash-conversion dependent, 8%.** A lower multiple now accompanies much stronger optical demand. The discount also has a concrete reason: the latest quarter's operating cash flow was negative while capex was heavy, with additional borrowing. The base assumes $20bn revenue, 20% normalized common margin and 220m diluted shares; it requires manufacturing scale, yields and cash to improve. No separate cash windfall or dividend is added. FN offers a cleaner assembly model; LITE's proposed merger changes its exposure. Catalyst: 1.6T/advanced optical demand converts to cash after the capacity build. Falsifiers: two successive quarters of major cash burn without a credible funded ramp, net leverage rising above roughly 3× normalized EBITDA, or structural margin pressure from architecture changes. This is the smallest selected weight because its return edge and confidence are weaker than the core positions.

### 5.3 Faster acceleration, delay and capex slowdown

**Acceleration:** the simultaneous bull endpoint is approximately +359% for the selected basket, versus +215% current and +312% core-and-satellite. It assumes successful deployment, high utilization, strong margins and supportive valuations. Faster capabilities can also accelerate substitution, regulation or obsolescence; the stock bull case is not synonymous with the fastest technological timeline.

**A 2.5-year delay:** a deliberately simple sensitivity compresses each stock's base terminal-wealth growth into the remaining 1.651 years and applies a 15% valuation haircut: `0.85 × (base wealth/current price)^((4.151−2.5)/4.151)`. The resulting cumulative outcomes are about **0% current, +17.9% concentrated and +13.2% core-and-satellite** through 2030. This is a mechanical wealth sensitivity, not a detailed production schedule or a new probability-weighted forecast. It does not mean the selected portfolio avoids a large interim drawdown. A delay accompanied by funding failure is much worse than this orderly deferral.

**A sharp AI-capex slowdown:** model an immediate 35–60% price shock to selected holdings: NVDA −50%, AVGO −45%, VRT −55%, CLS −60%, NVT −40%, NRG −35%, VST −40%, COHR −60%. The weighted portfolio loss is **−47.9%** before any rebound, dividends or tax effects. These are explicit hypothetical shocks, not estimated betas or a confidence interval. A long-term recovery requires that customers still have profitable workloads and balance sheets can fund the wait. The current architecture's simultaneous bear endpoint is worse than the concentrated basket's, largely because current price-to-normalized-value gaps are worse—not because concentrated portfolios are inherently safer.

For an apples-to-apples immediate shock across all architectures, apply the same company shocks wherever a stock appears. The additional assumptions are TSM/ASML/AMAT/GEV −45%; MU −60%; SNPS/CCJ/MSFT −30%; ANET/DELL/INTC −50%; CEG/PWR −35%; CRWD/GOOGL −25%; PLTR/MP −40%; AVAV −15%; KTOS −20%; MRNA −5%; BE −60%. This yields **−43.55% current, −47.90% concentrated, and −44.70% core-and-satellite**. Thus concentration is worse in this near-term capex shock even though its better starting valuations produce a less negative modeled 2030 bear endpoint. MRNA's mild assumed decline is not a pandemic-hedge forecast. The shock vector is a transparent thought experiment, not a covariance estimate or a liquidation instruction.

**Forecast fragility:** reducing all selected expected terminal wealth by 20% lowers the implied CAGR from 22.8% to approximately **16.4%**. A 30% haircut lowers it to about **12.7%**. A uniform terminal-multiple reduction is not identical to this total-wealth haircut, particularly for EV models with debt. The comparison nevertheless demonstrates that small confidence errors can consume most of the apparent advantage over a broad equity benchmark. The opportunity is substantial; the precision is not.

### 5.4 Why choose eight, not twenty-four or fifteen?

The current 24 positions are excessive **for this objective and these valuations**, not in some universal portfolio-theory sense. Small allocations often add tracking work and narrative comfort without materially hedging the dominant factor. MRNA and INTC are the clearest examples of labels masquerading as reliable hedges. The alternative fifteen is defensible for an investor who values smoother single-name exposure more highly, but its additional names do not improve expected return in this model.

The selected eight deliberately accept higher idiosyncratic concentration and shared AI-demand exposure for stronger entry-price economics. The default 15% cap binds NVDA/AVGO/VRT. Execution/financing-sensitive CLS/NVT/NRG are capped at 12%; aggregate merchant power is kept at 23%, leaving VST 11%; COHR receives the residual 8% because of weaker cash-conversion confidence. These are **judgmental permanent-loss limits**, not results of an empirically calibrated optimizer. They explain why the arithmetic highest-ranked NRG is not automatically 15% and why the final weights are not a mechanical return-ranked fill.

The choice is decisive but conditional. If the investor cannot tolerate a plausible 50–60% EHF loss, the correct answer is not to pretend that fifteen AI-related names eliminate the risk. It is to revisit the overall EHF risk budget with the investor. This assignment retains the prescribed approximately 50% overall allocation, and does not silently expand Foundation or change Continuity.

### 7.2 Scenario probabilities and uncertainty discipline

For long-horizon company underwriting, the default 25/50/25 bear/base/bull distribution is adjusted for fragile cases in the ledger. It represents a range of commercial outcomes, not an AGI-timeline probability. No sourced, reliable probability exists for “AGI in 2027,” a Taiwan blockade, or an engineered pandemic. Those events are analyzed as conditional stressors rather than assigned invented scientific odds.

A 100% common-equity loss is possible in a sufficiently severe tail. The model's positive bear values for mature companies are representative recession/competitive outcomes, not guaranteed floors. The zero-equity bears for selected excluded neoclouds demonstrate financing risk explicitly. To assess household resilience, stress both Event Horizon and Continuity together: a 60% EHF decline plus a 35% decline in the other 50% equity allocation would reduce net investments by approximately **47.5%**, before Foundation cash and any other assets. This is arithmetic, not a forecast.

The final portfolio is **not** an all-weather portfolio. It has no dedicated defense, privacy, bio-risk or geopolitical-insurance sleeve. That is a conscious opportunity-cost decision: those securities did not demonstrate reliable hedging payoffs or sufficiently attractive returns. The risk is accepted, not erased.

### 7.3 Monitoring and thesis invalidation

Review quarterly earnings, guidance, balance sheets, cash flow, fully diluted shares and customer concentration. Monitor order cancellations and conversion—not just announced orders—plus lead times, spot/contract pricing, utilization, credit terms and power commissioning. Separate organic growth from acquired revenue and product sales from financing income. Refresh the model after a material earnings/contract event or a 20% price change; there is no obligation to trade at every review.

For the portfolio thesis itself, three conditions would justify a broad downgrade by 2027: (a) capabilities keep improving but paid workloads fail to cover economic compute costs; (b) suppliers expand capacity so fast that bottleneck margins normalize well below these assumptions; or (c) financing/policy constraints stop the physical build even though demand exists. A two-year delay is survivable for a profitable, conservatively financed business; it can destroy common equity at a highly levered builder.

Rebalance at review dates if drift creates a position materially above 15%, using taxes and market conditions rather than a blind intraday rule. A deliberate target above 15% would require a new exceptional-evidence analysis; none is authorized by this report. Do not increase EHF's household share simply because the expected-return model looks attractive.

### 8.1 What is the complete AI datacenter bottleneck map?

**Prior May conclusion.** The physical stack was the organizing principle: leading-edge foundry, accelerators/custom silicon, memory, lithography and tools, EDA, networking, racks/cooling, power, electrical equipment, fuel and optics. The expanded portfolio treated many of these as simultaneous investable bottlenecks. The important insight was that model software cannot create operating megawatts, qualified chips or reliable interconnect on demand. The weakness was treating each bottleneck as deserving its own allocation.

**New evidence since May.** October/November operating releases show growth at several links simultaneously: TSM, ANET, VRT and COHR report substantial expansion; GEV's reservations/backlog indicate customer urgency; CLS provides a much larger 2027 outlook. But COHR's negative quarterly operating cash flow and heavy capex, IREN's remaining funding needs, and the equity financing at builders show that money and cash conversion are constraints too. The IEA's [October 14, 2026 integration study](https://www.iea.org/reports/powering-artificial-intelligence/executive-summary) adds current evidence that grid access, local policy and affordability shape deployment. These are fresher observations than the original AI timeline essays.

**Updated answer.** The full economic chain is: solvent end customer and useful paid workload → viable model/software → data rights, evaluation and security → financing and site rights → permits/water/grid or onsite generation → transmission/distribution/transformers/switchgear/UPS → thermal management → wafers/tools/materials → packaging/HBM → accelerators/custom silicon → racks/networking/optics/storage → commissioning/utilization → cash collection and reinvestment. Skilled labor, construction coordination and reliable suppliers cross several links. A missing link can idle everything downstream. Conversely, reducing compute per useful task can relieve several bottlenecks together.

Rank each link on four tests: how scarce is *qualified* capacity, how long does incremental supply take, who can increase price, and how readily can customers redesign or substitute? A queue or reservation is weaker evidence than a funded contract; a funded contract is weaker than commissioned capacity and collected cash. Avoid counting a hyperscaler's announced campus, its developer's announcement and its equipment supplier's order as three independent demand sources. Also distinguish one-time construction rent from recurring operating rent and technology-specific scarcity from a durable franchise.

**Portfolio implication.** Own eight attractive exposures, not every box: NVDA/AVGO 30%, VRT/NVT 27%, CLS/COHR 20%, VST/NRG 23%. TSM, ASML, MU and others remain central to the map but receive no direct allocation at this opportunity cost. Financing scarcity supports rejecting debt-heavy neoclouds rather than assuming lenders will always fund growth.

**Confidence: 4/5** in the dependency map; 3/5 in the location of 2030 excess profits. **Disconfirming evidence:** sustained falling equipment prices and delivery times alongside rising available capacity; project cancellations without replacement demand; or strong AI adoption with substantially less infrastructure spending per dollar of customer value than modeled. Any of those can leave the map technically correct and the stocks unattractive.

### 8.2 Hypothetical C15: What would an AGI/ASI do to maximize money?

**Prior May conclusion.** The answer emphasized compounding advantages from superior research, automation, financial decision-making and control of scarce physical inputs. Compute, power, data and real-world execution could turn cognitive superiority into economic leverage. That intuition supported owning infrastructure rather than trying to identify a single winning application.

**New evidence since May.** The October platform results show enormous commercial scale but also enormous cash commitments: Microsoft's reported cash spending on property/equipment rose sharply; Amazon's trailing free cash flow was negative; Meta's quarter also had negative free cash flow. These observations are not evidence that an autonomous system already maximizes wealth reliably. The [METR time-horizon page](https://metr.org/time-horizons/) also cautions against overinterpreting extrapolation; its September 8 update status and evaluation limitations make the original task-horizon slope an unsuitable proof of autonomous commercial competence. An older benchmark trend is not a live audit of a financially autonomous agent.

**Updated answer.** Within lawful, enforceable commercial arrangements, a highly capable system would seek the highest risk-adjusted return on incremental capital: automate expensive work, improve products, shorten discovery/design cycles, negotiate efficient resource contracts, and reinvest where competitors cannot quickly eliminate the advantage. It would distinguish accounting revenue from owner cash and avoid building capacity whose replacement cost exceeds its economic yield. It might prefer renting compute, acquiring an existing cash generator or selling useful software over owning a datacenter. Ownership of every physical bottleneck is not a logical necessity.

For an outside shareholder, the central issue is the division of surplus. Customers can capture productivity gains through lower prices; employees, governments, suppliers, creditors and new competitors can capture other portions. A software company can become vastly more useful while its incremental returns decline. Likewise, a supplier's strategic importance can induce its customers to vertically integrate or subsidize competitors. Agency, access to capital, authorization and execution remain meaningful constraints; this report assumes neither unconstrained autonomous action nor inevitability of a single winner.

**Portfolio implication.** Prefer businesses with demonstrable monetization and an attractive entry value. The eight-stock basket is a practical claim on selected cash flows, not a claim to own what an AGI would own. NVDA and AVGO win on current economics plus modelled normalized growth; CLS/NRG win partly on price. MSFT remains a credible 17.6% modeled alternative but loses the marginal comparison after capex and valuation. No trading bots, leverage, crypto or private model-lab interests are added.

**Confidence: 3/5.** Economic incentives are clearer than the behavior of hypothetical advanced systems. **Disconfirming evidence:** applications retain much more durable owner cash than infrastructure while customers commoditize hardware; or autonomous systems demonstrably deliver commercial work cheaply enough to reduce aggregate investment demand. Reallocate toward the actual cash-capturing layer after valuation analysis, not toward the most impressive demonstration.

### 8.3 C14: What are the top stocks for the 100 GW datacenter buildout?

**Prior May conclusion.** The portfolio favored the broad physical stack: TSM/NVDA/ASML/MU, cooling and rack integration, electrical equipment, nuclear/gas power, and optical networking. The 100 GW framing highlighted how demand at that scale could overwhelm current supply chains. It did not establish that all the required capacity would be financed, built and utilized before 2030.

**New evidence since May.** GEV's October backlog/reservation disclosure, VRT's October order/revenue expansion, CLS's October 2027 guidance, and the IEA's October integration report strengthen the case that buildout is real. They do not convert a multi-year pipeline into delivered demand. As a scale cross-check, the IEA's [April 16, 2026 report](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary), **STALE and not new since May**, projects roughly 950 TWh of worldwide datacenter electricity use in 2030. It must not be relabeled a fresh November forecast.

**Updated answer.** First define the units. A 100 GW *facility* load running at 85% utilization consumes about 745 TWh annually. A 100 GW *IT* load at 1.2 power-usage effectiveness and 85% utilization consumes about 894 TWh. At continuous nameplate operation, 100 GW alone is 876 TWh before any additional facility overhead. Therefore an incremental 100 GW IT buildout in one country by 2030 is an aggressive scenario relative to that older global energy projection, not the base case. Nameplate generating capacity, facility connection capacity, installed accelerator capacity and average delivered load are different quantities.

The best strategic suppliers and the best stocks are different rankings. TSM and ASML remain exceptionally difficult to substitute, but their modeled returns of 13.5% and 8.6% do not beat the chosen basket. VRT/NVT benefit before and during commissioning; CLS participates in system integration; NVDA/AVGO capture compute/networking economics; COHR benefits if optical intensity expands profitably; NRG/VST sell electricity and customer solutions. GEV has strategic turbine exposure but a much more demanding price and deposit-sensitive cash profile.

**Portfolio implication.** The valuation-adjusted shortlist is the selected eight, with the largest targets capped at 15%. The top modeled CAGRs are NRG/NVDA near 24.5%, followed by VRT/NVT/CLS around 23.5–23.7%; this ordering is too close to justify false precision. COHR has the smallest weight because its cash-conversion risk is more immediate. No allocation is justified solely by a 100 GW headline. A slower 30–50 GW pathway must still support owner returns.

**Confidence: 4/5** in the unit arithmetic and physical requirements; **2/5** in realization of an incremental 100 GW IT scenario by 2030. **Disconfirming evidence:** funded commissioning slips materially; average utilization remains low; power contracts are canceled; or customers achieve their useful-output targets with far less hardware. Those would reduce revenue assumptions even if announced campus capacity continued rising.

### 8.4 C16: What would an AGI/ASI do to maximize its own intelligence?

**Prior May conclusion.** More compute, better algorithms, stronger research tools and iterative self-improvement could form a reinforcing loop, making chips, power and infrastructure unusually valuable. The investment inference leaned toward physical scarcity as the constraint on recursive progress.

**New evidence since May.** The latest company releases support greater spending on compute and interconnect, but they do not demonstrate an uncontrolled recursive capability loop. Current [Epoch trend material](https://epoch.ai/trends) separates training-compute growth, installed capacity and price-performance improvements; its underlying Q2/earlier observations are **STALE (>30 days)**. METR's September update status and limits to measuring longer tasks caution against extending a short benchmark curve into an assured schedule for autonomous research. Improvements in model capability, reliable completion, inference efficiency and commercial usefulness are related but not identical.

**Updated answer.** The hypothetical system would allocate resources to the highest marginal improvement in reliable capability: better algorithms and experiments, higher-quality feedback, useful tools and memory, robust evaluation, specialized hardware where justified, and carefully selected additional compute. More parameters or more electricity need not be the best next investment. Scientific validity, error correction and reproducibility become more important as research tasks lengthen. Scarce experimental data and access to real-world laboratories can matter alongside digital compute.

This creates two competing investment effects. Cheaper inference can broaden use enough to increase total infrastructure demand; it can also reduce the amount of infrastructure required for a fixed task volume. Which effect dominates is an empirical question about demand elasticity, prices and new applications. A claim that efficiency *must* increase total energy demand is no stronger than a claim that it *must* destroy it. The portfolio needs rising paid utilization, not simply faster benchmark improvement.

**Portfolio implication.** Hold both general-purpose and custom-silicon exposure, rather than betting that one architecture serves all workloads forever. NVDA/AVGO total 30%, but both share exposure to customer budgets, TSM manufacturing and networking substitution. VRT/NVT/NRG/VST are less tied to a specific chip design, although still exposed to aggregate deployment. Do not add EDA or proprietary-data stocks merely because they participate in the intelligence feedback loop; their lower expected returns currently lose the capital competition.

**Confidence: 3/5.** The marginal-resource framework is robust; the future balance between algorithms, data and physical compute is not. **Disconfirming evidence:** algorithmic improvements consistently outpace new useful demand, depressing aggregate spend and utilization; a dominant architecture substantially bypasses today's networking/cooling intensity; or evaluations show capability progress stalling despite much greater spending. Each would require a different earnings path rather than a cosmetic change to the narrative.

### 8.5 C17: What strategy would an AGI/ASI use to beat China in the race to ASI?

**Prior May conclusion.** The geopolitical answer centered on a US/allied advantage in advanced compute, energy, capital, talent and supply-chain coordination. **Updated answer.** A lawful national competitiveness strategy would build reliable energy, high-quality research and training, resilient allied manufacturing, secure infrastructure, efficient permitting and capital markets, and verifiable standards for safe deployment. It would diversify critical dependencies, preserve international scientific and commercial cooperation where appropriate, and measure actual output rather than symbolic announcements. Competitive advantage depends on execution and institutions, not just access to a model. No confident current ranking of hypothetical ASI capability follows from public company earnings.

For investors, a successful industrial policy can still produce poor shareholder returns. Subsidies may support customers, jobs or strategic capacity without covering the cost of capital for minority owners. National-security intervention can restrict exports, cap profits, force investment or dilute shareholders. Conversely, a globally competitive supplier can remain profitable while its home government faces strategic vulnerabilities. Those facts are more informative for equity value than the generic statement that rare earths are strategic.

**Updated answer.** Critical minerals are one class of potential Achilles' heels, not a universal final constraint. Separate ore availability from economically recoverable reserves, processing capacity, purity, qualified components, logistics and geopolitical access. Gallium, germanium and indium are not interchangeable with magnet rare earths, and owning MP is not a comprehensive semiconductor-materials hedge. A small-volume material can stop production, but a high market price may also accelerate recycling, substitution, redesign and new supply. A shortage's social importance and the shareholder profit captured by one miner are not proportional.

There are also two clocks: a sudden export interruption can bind immediately, while new processing and qualification can take years. A four-year investor needs both interruption risk and the supply response. Government-backed price floors can improve project financing, but they do not guarantee the common stock's valuation, protect against dilution, or assure customers buy every unit produced. The contractual beneficiary and economic obligations matter more than the headline subsidy amount.

**Portfolio implication.** MP goes to 0%; the 3% funds NRG 2% and VST 1%. CCJ also goes to 0% for separate uranium valuation and contract reasons, not because uranium and rare earths share identical economics. No small basket of miners is added as a vague insurance sleeve. Materials remain on the dependency dashboard; if a specific publicly investable processor obtains durable high-return contracts at an attractive valuation, it can reenter the capital competition.

**Confidence: 4/5** that material-specific analysis is necessary; **3/5** in MP's exclusion at the current opportunity cost. **Disconfirming evidence:** substantial recurring magnet revenue with strong cash margins and limited new equity, enforceable contract economics that support superior owner returns, or a verified shortage for which an investable supplier has protected capacity and pricing. A rising commodity price alone is insufficient.

### 8.7 C6: How big a deal is the need for power, and which source benefits most?

**Prior May conclusion.** Power was the leading physical constraint. The portfolio spread exposure across existing nuclear and gas generation, grid construction, equipment, uranium and grid-bypass fuel cells. The conventional power-related sleeves totaled 29%, before counting VRT's thermal/electrical role. Nuclear scarcity and onsite alternatives received substantial narrative weight.

**New evidence since May.** VST's November release includes the completed Cogentrix acquisition and 2027 guidance, giving a clearer near-term operating perimeter. It is newer evidence than the April demand projection.

**Updated answer.** Power remains a major constraint, but location and timing dominate a simple national capacity total. Existing dispatchable capacity near usable interconnections can be valuable quickly. Gas can provide flexible supply where turbines, fuel and permits are available; existing nuclear offers high utilization but not instant new construction; renewables and storage can contribute rapidly in favorable locations, without being identical to continuous firm supply. Grid upgrades, demand flexibility, storage duration and onsite systems determine how these sources combine. No single technology supplies every incremental workload on the same timetable.

The most valuable asset is often *deliverable power at the right place and time*, not nameplate generation. Announced non-firm connection potential should not be counted as constructed generation or guaranteed energy. Retail and hedging contracts also determine which generator captures higher market prices; VST's largely hedged 2027 generation limits simplistic spot-price extrapolation. **Portfolio implication.** Use NRG 12% and VST 11% for merchant/retail generation economics. Add NVT 12% and retain VRT at 15% for the electrical/thermal chain: together these four are 50% of EHF, although only 23% is classified as generation. Remove CEG/PWR/GEV/CCJ/BE because their current prices and business-specific risks lose the return comparison, not because their products cease to matter. The accepted risk is much greater dependence on gas/merchant/retail economics and less pure nuclear/fuel exposure.

**Confidence: 4/5** in power's importance; **3/5** in which owners capture the surplus. **Disconfirming evidence:** persistent weak realized prices or customer margins, poor acquired-asset returns, outages, adverse binding policy changes, falling onsite-system costs that bypass the selected generators, or a clear reduction in required electricity per paid workload without compensating demand growth.

### 8.9 C12: Can the portfolio invest in proprietary data as a bottleneck?

**Updated answer.** Yes, proprietary data is investable through public equities, but the moat must be decomposed. The valuable combination is exclusive or difficult-to-replicate data, permission to use it, freshness, verification, embedded distribution, and a workflow that produces a measurable customer outcome. A static archive that a model can cheaply reconstruct is less attractive than continuously updated decision-critical information with auditability. PLTR's strength is integration and operating software; it should not be valued as though it owns every customer's private dataset. PLTR is removed from 2% to 0% because its entry valuation overwhelms the business improvement under this model. The absence of a data allocation is an opportunity-cost result, not a conclusion that data is free or irrelevant. If the final hardware basket becomes substantially more expensive, these recurring-cash businesses are plausible replacements.

**Confidence: 4/5** in the existence of investable data moats; **3/5** in their AI-driven acceleration. **Disconfirming evidence:** loss of exclusive rights, customer churn, commoditized access or weak pricing despite rising use; conversely, verified AI-driven retention, organic growth and owner cash materially above the model would falsify the current exclusion. ### 8.12 C7: How should the portfolio invest for a world where bio-engineered pandemics become easily accessible?

**Updated answer.** Treat the premise as a serious conditional risk, not an established universal fact. This report does not attempt to operationalize harmful biological activity. **Confidence: 4/5** that common-stock exposure is not reliable insurance; **2/5** in the timing/severity of future bio-risk. **Disconfirming evidence:** verified commercial approvals, reimbursement, scalable production and sustained owner cash that materially exceed the model, or a much lower price. ### 8.14 What could prove the bottleneck thesis wrong by 2027?

**New evidence since May.** Many stocks fell despite better reported earnings, proving that fundamental direction alone was insufficient at May prices. **Updated answer.** The strong version fails if profitable *paid demand* cannot support the capacity, if competitive supply eliminates supplier rents, or if financing/physical constraints prevent delivery within the investment horizon. AI can succeed socially while this portfolio fails financially. Conversely, a two-year delay need not destroy a conservatively financed franchise but can invalidate a highly levered capacity builder. Review the following decision rules rather than relying on a single benchmark or stock price:

| By-2027 test | Specific disconfirming observation | Model/action consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| End-user economics | Two major customers cut comparable AI-related capex plans by at least 15%, alongside weakening paid utilization or monetization | Reduce supplier revenue paths; freeze basket additions pending review. The 15% threshold is a chosen alert, not a sourced prediction. |

**Confidence: 4/5** that these are useful falsification tests; **3/5** that the current base case survives all of them. **Disconfirming evidence for the skeptical case:** several quarters of strong paid adoption, high utilization, cash collection and profitable incremental capacity without excessive dilution.
