## 1. Bottom Line

- **V10.2 holds eight stocks that supply the scarcest inputs to an AI-2027-style buildout:** chips, HBM memory, power, cooling, electrical gear, rack integration and site construction, plus META as ballast.
- **If AI-2027 lands within about a year of schedule, the fund ends 2030 at about 2.0–2.9×** (2.45× if exactly on time). That turns $100k into about $199k–$293k. If it runs 1.5–2 years late it still makes about 15% a year. Its weak spot is an AI-spending bust, where it loses about 62%.
- **Under your view (AI-2027 ±1 year), the fund's expected return is about 20% a year.** On the model's more cautious probabilities it is 15.5%.
- **Every holding trades below fair value:** 22–40% below under your view, 11–29% on the model's view. Today's prices assume AI lands somewhere between a stall and "AI-2027 late," so the on-time case is essentially free upside (Section 4).
- **Re-weighting toward your view barely changes the stock list.** The best names under your view are mostly the ones already held. The one clear improvement is **MOD**: 5% if its Nov 18 investor day passes the gate (already planned), then **up to 8%** once its next report confirms it.
- **AGI-focused investors mostly hold what the fund already owns:** memory, power and NVDA. The big new name is **Anthropic's IPO (ANT, trading Nov 19)**. On a rough screen it doesn't beat the holdings at the IPO price; at most it's a small Moonshot position (Section 9).
- **The AI-2027 compute workbook confirms the picks rather than changing them.** HBM is the binding input in every year from 2025 to 2030. The workbook leaves out power entirely, and power is the real wall it would hit.
- **Conviction averages 6.9/10.** NVDA and MU are highest at 8.5. CLS is lowest at 5. Nothing reaches 9–10, because every hardware holding loses 58–75% in a bust and several have unresolved near-term events.

## 6. How Conviction Is Scored

**Conviction** = how confident we are that this stock, *at this weight*, is the right choice compared with the alternatives. It is not a prediction that the stock won't fall.

| Part (0–2) | 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Edge** | Clearly beats the best unheld alternative in its role, under both views | Beats it, but within ~2pp, or under one view only | Doesn't beat it |
| **AI-2027 fit** | AI-2027 return ≥ 26% (1.5 for 23–26%) | 21–23% | < 20% (0.5 for 20–21%) |
| **Bottleneck** | Binding through 2030 and hard to substitute | Scarce but competitive | Not a supply bottleneck |
| **Evidence** | Hard data (orders, contracts, guidance), both models agree, no open event | Hard data, with one-model inputs or an open event | Mostly assumptions |
| **Downside** | Bust better than −40% | Bust −55% to −70%, delay better than −25% | Bust worse than −70% *and* delay worse than −30% (0.5 = one of the two) |

## 7. The AI-2027 Compute Workbook

### What it is

The workbook is "AI chip compute forecast" by Romeo Dean, the backend model for AI-2027's compute supplement ([Google Sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1Ko-olwjDy6h8rXLZBFpP-e2GibGqRhfV28S4v8xtzrM/edit?gid=1866551567)).

- The export downloaded Nov 11, 2026 has **one tab, "Clean version"** (rows 1–78, years 2021–2030, plus one chart). Every row, formula and note was read.
- It is a **supply model conditioned on high demand** ("similar to 2023 vs 2024"). Each year, AI chip output is the **minimum** of three supplies: CoWoS packaging, HBM memory, and leading-edge (≤5nm) wafers.
- It then derives the market size, compute, power and training-run size.

### What it projects

|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|

**Against reality in 2026:**

- **Spending is ahead.** The chip market is about $450–500B against the sheet's $396B. Memory makers' revenue is far above anything the sheet implies: SK hynix booked about ₩104.6T (~$78B) in Q3 2026 alone, though that is company-wide revenue, not HBM only.
- **Single-cluster scale is behind.** The sheet has a 5.6 GW training run in 2026; no coherent ~1 GW cluster has been verified yet.

### What it means for the portfolio

1. **HBM is the wall, every year.** HBM output grows 7.8× from 2026 to 2030, and its wafer demand (31.6M a year by 2030) exceeds the entire DRAM industry's output today.
    - **Supports MU at or near the cap**, and is the strongest argument for SK hynix (see Section 9, and its ADR problem).
2. **Chip volume grows about 67% a year.** That supports NVDA, and CLS, which assembles the racks.
3. **Power per chip rises 2.6× by 2027.** Liquid cooling and rack power become mandatory: **VRT, NVT and MOD.**
4. **Power is the missing constraint, and it's the real one.** The sheet's own chips would draw about 490 GW by 2030; its US-capacity row is only about 1,600 GW in total. An AI-2027 path is limited in practice by energized power, interconnection and buildable sites.
    - **That is where V10.2 holds 46%** (VRT, NVT, STRL, NRG). The sheet's 2030 training run (about 90 GW) is essentially Situational Awareness's 100GW cluster.
5. **Leading-edge wafers are the second wall, in 2029–30** (99% AI share by 2030). That is the case for TSM and ASML. But at today's prices they return only 14–15% under the owner view, below every holding, so they stay on re-entry triggers.
6. **Packaging is not the wall.** CoWoS capacity has about 2× slack in every year, so no packaging-only names are needed.

**Did it change the picks?** No new names. It confirms the existing ones, and supports two small tilts: MU from 14% to 15%, and the MOD gate. Both are in Section 10.

### Problems in the workbook (don't treat its dollars as forecasts)

- **HBM prices compound twice.** Each year's formula multiplies by 1.03^n, with n rising from 1 to 7. So the yearly price rise itself grows, from 3% in 2024 to 23% in 2030, and that produces the $306B. Growing units only from 2026 at +3% a year gives about **$180B**; the final document uses $165B from a 2024 anchor.
- **The packaging share is applied twice** in the chip-output formula (row 46). This doesn't change any output, because HBM binds.
- **Mislabeled units.** The datacenter power row (54) is in kW with no unit shown, and the training-run power row (71), labeled GW, is actually in kW. So the 2030 run is 89.6 GW, not "89.6M GW."
- **Chip size isn't adjusted consistently.** Chip counts are adjusted to H100 size in 2024 only; later years count full-size chips.
- **Copy-paste errors in the notes.** The note on Anthropic's chip share describes Meta's.
- **Correction for the final document:** Part II.C says V7.3's "25 Palo Verdes" figure is a 2028 number. In the sheet, **25 is the 2030 value** (row 75: 4.5 in 2027, 9.2 in 2028). The Codex deep dive's actual point was that the ">9" figure belongs to 2028. Fix this line when merging.

## 8. Why This Mix Under an AI-2027 View

1. **Every holding pays even if AI-2027 is late.** At 1.5–2 years late, every stock returns 1.6–2.1×. At 1 year late, 1.7–2.3×. The fund doesn't need AI-2027 to hit its dates.
2. **It owns the inputs, not the winners.** Chips, memory, power, cooling, electrical and sites get bought whichever lab wins.
3. **Re-optimizing for the owner view lands in almost the same place.** Among fully valued names that don't go to zero in a bust, the holdings rank: STRL #2, NVDA #3, MU #4, NRG #5, CLS #6, VRT #7, NVT #8–9 (tied with AVGO), META #10.
    - The only higher-ranked name not held is MOD (#1), and it's gated.
    - AVGO ties NVT on return but has a much worse bust (−73% vs −58%).
4. **The faster-path stocks that score higher go to zero in a bust.** CoreWeave and the miners turned AI hosts return 3.5–7.4× on the fast paths but lose 100% in the bust.
5. **Dropping META for a faster name doesn't pay:**

*META's job is to cut the bust loss, and it does that at almost no cost to return, even under the owner view.*

### SK hynix (SKHY): the biggest gap, with a catch

- **The case:** the workbook gives SK hynix 45% of HBM, against Micron's 10%.

    - Q3 2026 revenue was about ₩104.6T, with roughly an 80% operating margin, and it plans about ₩100T of shareholder returns for 2026.
    - Reported forward P/E is about 4.5–5.7× 2027 earnings; sources differ on the price basis. Aschenbrenner's fund has reportedly traded it since September.
- **The catch: the ADR premium.** On Nov 9 the ADR closed at $134.38, which is $1,344 per underlying share. The Seoul share was ₩1,136,000 ≈ $834 at 1,362.7 KRW/USD. **The ADR cost about 61% more for the same share.** The premium has been 3% to 61% since listing, and reports say two-way conversion may be opening, which could narrow it.
- **Rough proxy (not a full valuation):** at the Seoul price, SK hynix's economics look similar to MU's (13% model E; 2.7× in AI-2027). Through the ADR, the result depends on what happens to the premium by 2030:

| Premium by 2030 | Expected (proxy) | AI-2027 (proxy) |

| Disappears | ~1%/yr | ~13%/yr |
| Falls to 10% | ~3%/yr | ~16%/yr |
| Stays at 61% | ~13%/yr | ~27%/yr |

- **Verdict:** owning HBM through MU is better than SKHY at this premium.
    - **Trigger:** a premium below about 15%, *or* access to Seoul shares through a broker with Korea Exchange access.
    - Then fully value SK hynix as a candidate to share the HBM position with MU.
