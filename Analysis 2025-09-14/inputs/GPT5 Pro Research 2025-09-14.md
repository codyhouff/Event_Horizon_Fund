# **AI-2027 Event Horizon Fund – Final Allocation & 2026–2030 Outlook**

## **Executive Summary**

The **Event Horizon Fund** (approximately 50% of total capital) is being optimized to maximize returns under the “AI-2027” scenario – a world where **exponential AI compute demand collides with hard physical constraints**. The revised portfolio concentrates on the **critical bottlenecks** in this AI boom: power generation, advanced chip manufacturing capacity, high-end semiconductor equipment, memory (HBM), networking, and cooling. These are the choke points with **durable pricing power** as AI demand outstrips supply*\[1\]\[2\]*. We add selective upstream exposures (EDA software, strategic materials) to hedge supply-chain risks, and we maintain a 10% Moonshot sleeve for asymmetric tail outcomes.

**Base-Case Performance:** If the AI-2027 scenario plays out as expected, the Event Horizon Fund is projected to **compound at very high rates through the late 2020s**, roughly **+35–45% annual returns in the next 2–3 years, tapering to ~15–20% by 2029–2030** as the build-out matures*\[3\]*. This trajectory would approximately **quadruple** the fund’s value by 2030. The biggest contributors to performance will be stocks that control the **binding supply constraints**: e.g. **Constellation Energy** in nuclear power (forecasting ~50–60% annual surges in peak years)*\[4\]*, **Micron** in memory (~+70% in supply-crunch years)*\[5\]*, **NVIDIA** in AI chips (~+60% late-decade)*\[4\]*, and **Vertiv** in data center cooling (~+55–60%)*\[4\]*. These names anchor the portfolio and drive its upside. The end result is a more concentrated, high-conviction fund targeting **optimal total return** if our AI boom thesis comes to fruition.

## **AI-2027 Scenario: Demand Explosion vs. Physical Limits**

Under the AI-2027 base-case scenario, **demand for AI compute grows at unprecedented rates, while key enablers face inelastic supply**. For example, state-of-the-art AI chip production is projected to grow ~80% CAGR (from ~7.7M units in 2025 to 92M+ in 2030)*\[6\]*, and the AI chip market value soars from \$255B in 2025 to ~\$3T in 2030*\[7\]*. **However, supply of critical inputs lags badly:** leading-edge wafer capacity (\<5 nm) expands only ~16% CAGR*\[1\]*, and advanced packaging capacity (CoWoS) about ~71% CAGR*\[8\]* – not enough to catch up with chip demand. By 2030, **AI could consume virtually 100% of all \<5 nm foundry output***\[9\]*, effectively cannibalizing other tech industries. This extreme supply-demand divergence gives monopoly suppliers like TSMC enormous pricing power.

The most acute bottleneck is **power**. AI data centers’ power consumption is on track to hit 434 GW by 2030 (108% CAGR)*\[2\]* – a **“physics limit”** constraint, given new power plants and grid infrastructure take years to build. Securing 24/7 electricity (especially carbon-free) becomes a strategic imperative. Likewise, **memory (HBM)** and **advanced cooling** emerge as hard limits: each new NVIDIA GPU generation consumes more silicon area, more memory stacks, and **far more watts**, creating a *“compounding scarcity”* feedback loop*\[10\]*. In short, the scenario is one of **explosive demand slamming into constrained supply** across chips, materials, and energy. This portfolio is designed to own the **choke-point assets** that everyone will bid up as AI demand outstrips supply – the foundries, equipment makers, power producers, etc., rather than the “end-product” buyers of scarce GPUs*\[11\]*.

## **Portfolio Strategy: Focus on Bottlenecks & Durable Profit Pools**

To capitalize on this environment, the Event Horizon Fund (EHF) is structured into thematic **“sleeves”**, each targeting a critical layer of the AI value chain where supply is tight and pricing power is high. Roughly 80–90% of the fund is allocated to these high-conviction core themes, with **deliberate avoidance of over-crowded trades** (we are *not* chasing the highest-flying AI software or model developers, nor over-weighting expensive GPU stocks). Instead, we favor the upstream “picks and shovels” supplying the AI arms race, especially those with **oligopolistic markets or irreplaceable assets***\[12\]*. The remaining ~10–20% of the fund rounds out exposure to governance and security (for AI operational risks) and holds a **Moonshot sleeve (10%)** of defined-risk option positions for asymmetric upside (this tail-risk sleeve is kept capped and separate, so it cannot drag down the core more than its allotted 10%). The Moonshot positions target infrequent but massive payoffs (e.g. a breakthrough by Intel, a major AI cloud deal for our crypto miners, a defense contract for autonomous drones, or an AI-accelerated pandemic scenario) while **capping downside to that 10%***\[13\]*.

**Key strategic shifts in this optimized portfolio:** We have **increased concentration** in the highest-conviction bottleneck areas – **Energy (power generation)**, **Semiconductor Capital Equipment**, and **Data Center Thermal/Integration (cooling)** – where new data underscored even greater scarcity and pricing power than previously thought*\[14\]\[15\]*. We also introduced two *new upstream sleeves* in prior revisions – **Electronic Design Automation (EDA)** and **Strategic Materials** – to hedge indispensable inputs (chip design software, and rare-earth minerals) that were not previously covered well. Finally, we **shifted the data center “servers” allocation toward established integrators** (like Dell) and away from smaller OEMs, aligning with the market’s transition from a “speed-to-market” phase (where niche players thrived) to a **“scale-of-deployment” war best won by large incumbents with global supply chains***\[16\]\[17\]*. In practice, this meant replacing a prior position in Super Micro (SMCI) with Dell, and trimming secondary server/component names (HPE, Flex, nVent) to double-down on the leaders (Vertiv and Dell)*\[18\]\[19\]*. The end result is a more **concentrated and simplified portfolio**: we pruned several small positions and reallocated into our top convictions, pushing many of those to ~5–15% weights (while respecting a 15% cap per stock).

**Risk posture:** This portfolio is aggressive in pursuit of the AI thesis – it is **not** diversified by normal standards (it’s heavily tech-centric and even within tech, focused on hardware/infra). However, it is diversified *within the AI build-out’s value chain*, spanning from upstream R&D tools to electricity to defense. If the AI megatrend materializes as expected, these picks should outperform broad markets by a wide margin. If the thesis breaks (e.g. geopolitical disruption, AI demand falters), the portfolio would suffer, but we have some built-in hedges (e.g. **MP Materials** as a geopolitical hedge, **Moderna** as a bio-risk hedge, *and the Moonshot sleeve’s* put-spread structures in some cases). Overall, the allocation tilts toward where we see **the most durable, non-substitutable profit pools** in an AI-saturated economy.

## **2026–2030 Performance Outlook by Theme and Top Holdings**

Even within a generally booming scenario, we expect **certain themes to vastly outperform in specific years**, corresponding to when their constraint bites hardest. Below we outline each core sleeve’s allocation, role, and expected performance trajectory under the AI-2027 base case:

- **Foundry & Advanced Packaging – 12% of EHF (TSMC – 12%)\**
  *Thesis:* TSMC is the *physical gatekeeper* of AI progress – virtually all cutting-edge AI chips must be made using TSMC’s leading-edge nodes *and* its advanced 2.5D packaging (CoWoS). As AI demand explodes, TSMC’s capacity remains scarce. By 2029–30, **AI could consume \>90% of all \<5 nm wafer output***\[20\]*, forcing other industries aside and allowing TSMC to charge premium prices. CoWoS packaging capacity is growing fast (~71% CAGR) but still lags chip demand, **ensuring a chronic shortage***\[8\]* that only TSMC (monopoly provider of CoWoS) can solve.\
  *Performance:* We expect TSMC to deliver **strong, steady gains** throughout the period as it monetizes this imbalance. In big AI ramp years (e.g. 2027 or 2029), TSM could see ~40%+ returns*\[21\]*. Even in “slower” years, high demand from hyperscalers keeps growth in the mid-teens. By 2030, TSMC should still be growing earnings as it brings 2 nm online. **Key drivers:** hyperscaler capex cycles and continued node shrinks (N3 → N2). **Risks:** Geopolitics (Taiwan) and any missteps by TSM or breakthrough by an alternative (e.g. Intel, though unlikely near-term). Conviction remains *very high* – we kept TSM at a full **12% weight** (unchanged) as a foundational holding. It may not be the fastest horse, but it’s one of the **surest bottleneck bets** in the entire AI theme*\[22\]*.

- **Semiconductor Equipment (WFE) – 11% (ASML – 8%, AMAT – 3%)\**

Every new fab in the world (whether in the US, EU, or Asia) must buy tools from the **same tiny oligopoly** of WFE companies*\[23\]*. The AI chip boom plus government onshoring (CHIPS Act, etc.) is triggering construction of many parallel fabs globally, *all* dependent on ASML, Applied Materials, Lam, KLA, etc.*\[24\]\[25\]*. We expect WFE demand to be robust for many years, and these companies’ pricing power to rise (e.g. ASML’s EUV machines are essentially a **100% monopoly** product with years-long waitlists*\[26\]*).\
  *Performance:* We simplified this sleeve to **focus on the top two** names: **ASML** (8%) and **Applied Materials** (3%). ASML, as the sole EUV provider, should have **outsize growth** – we project ~30–40% returns in the next couple of years (as High-NA EUV is adopted) and still ~15–20% p.a. by 2029–30*\[27\]*. Applied Materials provides a more diversified (and U.S.-centric) equipment exposure; we expect ~25–35% annual gains in the early years tapering to mid-teens later*\[28\]*. We **removed Lam Research and KLA** (previously 2% + 1%) to concentrate our bet on ASML’s unique monopoly and AMAT’s broad portfolio – this increases our **weight in the WFE sleeve from 10% to 11%**, with the incremental emphasis on ASML*\[29\]\[30\]*. Both ASML and AMAT are poised to benefit from the multi-year fab expansion and the **arms race for capacity** (e.g., ASML’s order book remains at record highs). **Risks:** Export restrictions or a tech slowdown; we monitor for any non-EUV lithography breakthroughs (very unlikely)*\[31\]*.

In the meantime, even base demand (EVs, wind turbines, and defense needs for rare earths) supports its growth. **No major changes** were needed here; we considered it appropriately sized at 3%. We simply note that MP is now a **core holding to hedge the “kill-switch” risk** inherent in our otherwise Asia-exposed hardware bets*\[81\]\[78\]*.

- **Moonshot (Tail-Risk) Sleeve – 10% (Various LEAP options and small-cap plays)\**
  *Thesis:* The Moonshot sleeve remains **10% of the Event Horizon Fund** (unchanged), dedicated to high-upside, *controlled-risk* positions that pay off in extreme scenarios. By design, each position here is sized and structured such that our **max loss is the sleeve’s 10%** (we use debit call spreads, long-dated options, etc., so we can’t lose more than we put in)*\[13\]*. The intent is to capture the **convex upside** of certain tail events or underdog outcomes without jeopardizing the core portfolio. Current Moonshot components include: **Intel (3.5%) LEAP call spreads** – a bet on a surprising Intel foundry comeback aided by CHIPS Act subsidies (pays off big if Intel’s new nodes succeed by 2026–27); **Crypto AI Hosting (3.5%)** across CoreWeave/Compute North-related plays (CORZQ, IREN, APLD) – plays on the thesis that ex-Bitcoin datacenters get repurposed for AI cloud capacity, with major hosting contracts (“LTAs”) potentially re-rating these equities 10×*\[82\]*; **Kratos additional calls (2.0%)** beyond the equity – levered bet on it winning big chunks of the Pentagon’s autonomous drone programs (Collaborative Combat Aircraft); and **Moderna calls (1.0%)** – a bit of extra convexity in case of an AI-heightened pandemic. We periodically adjust these positions and ladder expirations, but they share the trait of **low probability, high impact payoffs**.\
  *Performance:* In *base-case years*, we assume the Moonshot sleeve will have a slight **drag of perhaps –0.3% to –1.0% on the total portfolio** (this represents option premium decay if none of the tail events hit)*\[83\]*. This is acceptable “insurance cost.” However, in the event one of our theses hits, the sleeve can **deliver a windfall**. For example, our analysis suggests that: an Intel success headline could add **+3–8%** to the total portfolio’s value (as our call spreads revalue)*\[84\]*; a major AI hosting deal or funding for our data center plays could add **+4–12%***\[85\]*; a big defense contract for Kratos could add a few points; and a pandemic shock could add **+4–10%** via Moderna*\[86\]*. These would be on top of core returns. We are **keeping the Moonshot sleeve structure intact** – using mostly LEAP call spreads (pay ~35–45% of the spread width, aiming for 3–6× payouts) and some equity in the miners – and we **do not count on Moonshot in our base forecast***\[87\]*. It’s purely upside optionality. The critical part is that this sleeve is ring-fenced at 10% – even if all these bets expire worthless, the core 90% of the fund drives our results. Conversely, if a tail event occurs, this sleeve ensures we participate in the upside rather than just suffering the downside of, say, an AI supply shock or geopolitical surprise.

**Portfolio Return Drivers:** Based on the above, the Event Horizon Fund is positioned such that different sleeves drive different phases of the AI rollout. In **2026–27**, as the first big AI capacity surge hits, we expect **power and cooling** to be major drivers – e.g. **Vertiv** and **Constellation Energy** could each roughly **double by 2026–27***\[41\]\[5\]*, and **Dell** and **Quanta** should also see big early jumps (50%+). **NVIDIA** and **Broadcom**, coming off already high bases, may “only” rise ~15–30% in 2026*\[57\]\[88\]* before re-accelerating later. **Micron** could spike in 2027 (~+70%*\[5\]*) once new AI hardware launches strain memory supply. By **2028–29**, as huge data centers come online, **networking and second-wave capacity** plays step up: we see **Arista** and **Broadcom** gaining ~45%+ around 2028–29*\[33\]*, and **Micron** possibly another big jump (~+69% in 2029)*\[33\]*. **NVIDIA** regains momentum with new product cycles (potential ~+60% in 2029)*\[33\]*. Meanwhile, **TSMC, ASML, and AMAT** should steadily contribute ~30–40% in the mid-period then ~15–20% late – not top of the leaderboard, but given their weight, names like TSM and ASML will be among the **top contributors to total portfolio value each year***\[89\]*. Come **2030**, constraints tighten again (power and HBM “encore”), so we anticipate **another strong year for power and memory** – e.g. **Micron ~+64%, Constellation ~+50% in 2030***\[4\]*, keeping returns elevated even at the decade’s end.
