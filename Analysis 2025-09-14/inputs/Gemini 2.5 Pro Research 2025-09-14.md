## Section 2: The AI-2027 Scenario: A Unified Market Forecast (2025-2030)

### 2.1. The Physics of Exponential Growth

The investment environment from 2025 through 2030 is defined by a historic and widening chasm between the exponential demand for AI compute and the physically constrained supply of the infrastructure required to create it. This structural imbalance is the primary driver of the investment thesis.<sup>2</sup> The forecast data reveals a market scaling at an unprecedented rate. Total production of SOTA AI chips is projected to grow at an 80% CAGR, from 7.66 million units in 2025 to over 92 million units by 2030. This explosion in volume translates directly into value, with the total AI chip market size projected to expand from \$255 billion in 2025 to over \$3 trillion by 2030, an 85% CAGR. This indicates that immense value is being captured by the chip designers and their supply chain, rather than being competed away through price erosion.<sup>2</sup>

This demand surge collides with a series of hard physical constraints. The capacity for Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate (CoWoS) advanced packaging dedicated to AI is forecast to grow at a 77% CAGR, while High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) production grows at a 76% CAGR. While these growth rates are aggressive, they consistently lag the 80% CAGR in chip demand, ensuring a structurally tight market and sustained pricing power for the oligopolies that control these technologies.<sup>2</sup>

### 2.3. Key Dynamics & Second-Order Effects

A deeper analysis of the forecast reveals several powerful feedback loops and second-order effects that are critical to understanding the investment landscape.

#### 2.3.1. "The Power Wall" as the Terminal Bottleneck

The most severe constraint revealed by the data is power. The metric for "Total AI datacenter power demand" exhibits the highest growth rate in the entire forecast, a staggering 108% CAGR, expanding from 17.06 GW in 2025 to 434.66 GW by 2030.<sup>2</sup> This represents the ultimate physical bottleneck. The rate of electricity demand growth is faster than the rate of compute unit growth (80%), implying each new generation of chip is significantly more power-hungry. The supply response for power—new nuclear plants and high-voltage transmission lines—is measured in decades, while the supply response for silicon is measured in years.<sup>1</sup> Therefore, even if all semiconductor bottlenecks are eventually resolved, the entire AI buildout will be gated by the rate at which new, reliable power can be brought online. This dynamic elevates the Energy sleeve (CEG, PWR) to the highest strategic importance, as it controls the longest-lead-time, most inelastic resource in the entire stack.

#### 2.3.2. "Compounding Scarcity" Driven by Chip Design

The very innovation roadmap of the market leader, NVIDIA, actively exacerbates these core physical bottlenecks. New SOTA chips like the Blackwell (B100) and Rubin (R100) generations are physically larger, consuming more of a scarce wafer per unit. They also require more HBM stacks per chip and consume significantly more power.<sup>1</sup> This product strategy creates a "compounding scarcity" dynamic: each generational leap in performance simultaneously intensifies the demand for silicon area, HBM, and power. This creates a powerful, self-reinforcing feedback loop that directly benefits the suppliers of these scarce inputs—TSMC, Micron, and Constellation Energy—as their customers' own innovation roadmaps guarantee sustained demand and pricing power.

#### 2.3.3. "The Great Cannibalization" of the Semiconductor Industry

The forecast reveals a dynamic far more profound than simple market growth: the cannibalization of the entire leading-edge semiconductor industry by AI. The "Share of \<5nm wafer production used for AI chips" is projected to skyrocket from just 14.11% in 2025 to an astonishing 99.48% by 2030.<sup>2</sup> This indicates that AI is not merely an additive market; it is on a trajectory to consume the entire available supply of the most advanced manufacturing capacity on the planet. This will have powerful second-order effects, progressively crowding out traditional leading-edge customers like those in the premium smartphone and PC markets. The foundry's pricing power, therefore, is derived not just from the AI boom itself but from its strategic position as the ultimate arbiter of who gets access to the future of silicon, pitting all high-tech industries against each other for a resource pool being monopolized by AI.<sup>1</sup>

Recommendation: Maintain a high-conviction 12% allocation to TSM.

### 3.2. Accelerators & Custom Silicon

**Analysis:** The V6 sleeve is correct in its thesis but diluted in its composition. The core principle is to own the dominant, value-capturing platforms. NVIDIA (NVDA) is the undisputed dominant platform, with an 80-90% market share and a deep software moat in CUDA.<sup>1</sup> Broadcom (AVGO) has emerged as the clear \#1 enabler of the custom ASIC trend, a critical and growing segment of the market. Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) is a credible second-source supplier, which is crucial for de-risking the ecosystem but does not afford it the same pricing power or value capture as the market leader. ARM Holdings (ARM) operates a lower-beta royalty model that is less directly exposed to the physical bottlenecks that drive the core thesis.<sup>1</sup> The recommendation from the Gemini V7 analysis to consolidate this sleeve into the two primary value-capture nodes—the \#1 general-purpose platform (NVDA) and the \#1 custom silicon enabler (AVGO)—is strategically sound.<sup>2</sup>

Recommendation: Remove AMD and ARM. Increase the allocation to AVGO to 3% to reflect the growing importance of the custom silicon trend. Trim the NVDA allocation slightly to 8% to free up capital for more fundamental, higher-conviction upstream bottlenecks.

### 3.3. HBM/Memory

**Analysis:** The V6 thesis for Micron Technology (MU) is upgraded. The quantitative forecast confirms a persistent, albeit narrow, deficit between HBM demand growth (80% CAGR) and supply growth (76% CAGR), ensuring a structurally tight market.<sup>1</sup> Critically, the forecast that 98% of HBM will be used for AI chips by 2026 marks a structural de-risking of the business model, transforming HBM from a cyclical consumer-driven commodity to a secular AI-driven strategic component, which should lead to a fundamental re-rating of valuation multiples.<sup>1</sup> Micron's strong execution on its HBM3E roadmap and rapid market share gains are confirmed by recent analyst commentary, which highlights explosive earnings growth projections and a discounted valuation relative to peers.<sup>8</sup>

## Section 4: Fortifying the Foundation: Integrating New Upstream Bottlenecks

A major strategic enhancement to the portfolio is the introduction of three new sleeves that capture value further upstream in more fundamental and defensible bottlenecks. These were identified as critical omissions in the V6 portfolio.

### 4.1. Wafer Fab Equipment (WFE)

**Rationale:** The V6 portfolio is missing the "factories that build the factories." The WFE oligopoly represents a more fundamental bottleneck than the foundries themselves.<sup>1</sup> This thesis is amplified by a "geopolitical multiplier": the global race for supply chain sovereignty, fueled by government subsidies like the U.S. CHIPS Act, is forcing the construction of multiple, parallel fabs worldwide. Every one of these new fabs must be filled with equipment from the same WFE oligopoly, dramatically expanding their total addressable market. The very geopolitical risk that threatens TSM acts as a powerful demand driver for WFE providers.

- **ASML (8%):** ASML holds a 100% monopoly in EUV lithography, the technology required to manufacture all advanced AI chips.<sup>1</sup> Its position is arguably the most defensible in the entire AI value chain. The company's Q2 2025 financial results show strong total net sales of €7.7 billion and a robust order backlog, with EUV systems accounting for 48% of net system sales.<sup>34</sup> This position will anchor the new WFE sleeve.

- **AMAT (4%):** Applied Materials provides diversified exposure to the rest of the powerful WFE oligopoly, with market leadership in critical deposition and etch processes. Crucially, its large U.S. manufacturing footprint makes it a primary beneficiary of CHIPS Act-related onshoring, serving as a valuable US-centric hedge against the portfolio's significant Asia-based geopolitical risk.<sup>1</sup>

### 4.2. Electronic Design Automation (EDA)

**Rationale:** The V6 portfolio is missing the "digital blueprints." The EDA market is a stable duopoly, dominated by Synopsys and Cadence, that provides the essential software for all advanced chip design. They function as a high-margin, recurring-revenue "toll road" on the entire industry.<sup>1</sup> This position benefits from the "custom silicon multiplier": the rise of custom ASICs from hyperscalers, while a threat to NVIDIA, is a massive market expander for EDA companies. Every tech giant designing its own chip becomes a new, high-value EDA customer, allowing the portfolio to profit from the explosion in AI silicon design without taking on the specific competitive risk of which chip designer ultimately wins.

- **SNPS (5%):** Synopsys is the market leader. Its Q3 2025 financial results showed strength in its core Design Automation segment, which grew 23% year-over-year, despite weakness in its more cyclical IP business.<sup>36</sup> This position will anchor the new EDA sleeve.

### 4.3. Strategic Materials

**Rationale:** The entire semiconductor thesis rests on a vulnerable foundation: the critical minerals supply chain. The V6 portfolio has a severe, unhedged vulnerability to the "kill switch" risk of a supply chain disruption, as the U.S. is almost entirely import-reliant on China for key refined materials like Gallium (98%).<sup>1</sup> This risk is too significant to ignore.

- **MP (3%):** MP Materials is the leading US-based, vertically integrated rare earth producer and a designated "national champion" with direct Department of Defense backing. The company has secured a multi-billion-dollar partnership with the DoD that includes a \$400 million equity investment and long-term offtake agreements, providing immense revenue visibility and de-risking its strategy.<sup>1</sup> The company's Q2 2025 earnings confirmed this transformative partnership.<sup>39</sup> This position provides a direct hedge against the portfolio's most critical vulnerability.
